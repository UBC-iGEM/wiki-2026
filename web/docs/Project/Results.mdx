# <a id="c35ff9d2d0fb4f44ae83c67c7436d2f8" />Bronze Medal

## <a id="922118c51a454428a0aa0eecdfd76ace" />1. Competition Deliverables

<a id="ebce8792566f4748b712a36b84f78bb5" />Over the 2026 season, UBC Vancouver iGEM developed *FAST*, a microfluidic platform that determines whether bacteria in a patient sample can inactivate a candidate antibiotic, using engineered *E. coli* biosensors as the readout. The platform is antibiotic- and infection-agnostic by design, and we are validating it first on urinary tract infections. We completed every Competition Deliverable required for Bronze: our Safety Forms, Team Wiki, Judging Form, Presentation Video, and Judging Session.

## <a id="22d95e7caa644cdfb81f499a1e492768" />2. Project Attributions

<a id="734743e17fbb4d2886f7e9e975896ea8" />Throughout the season our members and executive team tracked individual contributions to *FAST*. We recorded what each team member did alongside the work contributed by advisors, sponsors, faculty, and our integrated Human Practices experts. Our completed Attributions Form was submitted through the Deliverables Dashboard and is publicly viewable on our team page, which is where judges should consult it; teams are no longer required to embed the form on the wiki.

# <a id="d6d62cc9f42c46eea7e511fb55d23041" />Silver Medal

## <a id="80c94586c4c744b9aea0cde9d4b0f1b2" />1. Engineering Success

<a id="6201c9032bac40979adc83d27bed4ea8" />Standard URL: [2026.igem.wiki/ubc-vancouver/engineering](https://2026.igem.wiki/ubc-vancouver/engineering)

<a id="d4043655d362454497075f313bfd0175" />*FAST* was built through repeated Design-Build-Test-Learn cycles in every subteam, summarised on our [Engineering Success](/ubc-vancouver/project/engineering-success) page. In the wet lab, the [Biosensor](/ubc-vancouver/wet-lab/biosensor) work moved from reporter selection through transformation and fluorescence validation to MIC titration, each round informing the next construct, while the [Patient Simulant](/ubc-vancouver/wet-lab/patient-simulant) work iterated on a representative sample matrix. In hardware, the [Microfluidic Chip](/ubc-vancouver/hardware/microfluidic-chip), [Bioreactor](/ubc-vancouver/hardware/bioreactor), and [Fluorescence Detector](/ubc-vancouver/hardware/fluorescence-detector) each went through successive build-and-test rounds. Modelling fed back into these cycles through the [Degradation Model ](/ubc-vancouver/modelling/degradation-model-), [PRISM](/ubc-vancouver/modelling/prism), [Promoter Library](/ubc-vancouver/modelling/promoter-library), and [Patient Sample Design Of Experiments](/ubc-vancouver/modelling/patient-sample-design-of-experiments) pages.

## <a id="8607f6392f5c4f77bb5432ee9b145ae9" />2. Human Practices

<a id="2eefa6984dcd4418b36eb275c939118a" />Standard URL: [2026.igem.wiki/ubc-vancouver/human-practices](https://2026.igem.wiki/ubc-vancouver/human-practices)

<a id="73fdb314cfb44730ac3285fa47cecaea" />Asking whether a rapid susceptibility test is responsible and good for the world meant asking who it would reach and who it would leave out. Our  work began with a structured [Investigation 1 - Literature Review](/ubc-vancouver/human-practices/investigation-1---literature-review) and continued into [Investigation 2 - integrated Human Practices (iHP)](/ubc-vancouver/human-practices/investigation-2---integrated-human-practices-ihp), then opened into a broader dialogue about the barriers patients face, documented across [Barrier 1 - Companionship Support](/ubc-vancouver/human-practices/barrier-1---companionship-support) and [Barrier 2 - Informational Support ](/ubc-vancouver/human-practices/barrier-2---informational-support-). That dialogue produced our [Demystifying AMR](/ubc-vancouver/human-practices/demystifying-amr) work and our [Education](/ubc-vancouver/human-practices/education) programming, and [Our Journey](/ubc-vancouver/human-practices/our-journey) records how our thinking changed.

<a id="a8a55b0964e3426abfd96bf992a7f2ed" />The project changed as a result. Our interview with Dr. R.E.W. (Bob) Hancock, Professor of Microbiology and Immunology at UBC and Director of the Centre for Microbial Diseases and Immunity Research, is one example. He pointed out that resistance usually goes unnoticed, because when an antibiotic fails the clinician switches drugs and no one records why. We added β-lactams to our testing panel, planned for multi-antibiotic testing, and moved our initial validation target to UTIs, where urine is easy to collect and where BC pharmacists have been prescribing without laboratory confirmation since 2023.

# <a id="99a1babfeac94cf2b43f6d158b1e91fc" />Gold Medal

## <a id="9143c0d284204d2285bde82c350a358d" />1. Excellence in Synthetic Biology

<a id="d9ccab3aa02e4d5686355f5e052a1d68" />We selected **Best Measurement** from General Bio Engineering, and **Best Hardware** and **Best Integrated Human Practices** from Specializations. There is no separate Gold vote in 2026; the criterion is met only if all three awards reach the performance standard on the judges' rubrics.

### <a id="7d0daede78e14527b76c2be314c0df8d" />Best Measurement

<a id="42d73906c08440f7be27f084f9bf3528" />Standard URL: [2026.igem.wiki/ubc-vancouver/measurement](https://2026.igem.wiki/ubc-vancouver/measurement)

<a id="06abfb9766fd4af29926cb7ca04f8382" />Measurement is the readout the whole platform depends on. The diagnostic compares the fluorescence fingerprint of a panel of stress-responsive *E. coli* biosensors against a reference, so a result means something only if that fluorescence can be measured reproducibly and read against controls. Our [Best Measurement](/ubc-vancouver/project/best-measurement) page covers the biosensor characterisation, the MIC titration that established the subinhibitory window for both reporter strains, and the controls we used to separate signal from baseline. The [Fluorescence Detector](/ubc-vancouver/hardware/fluorescence-detector) page documents the low-cost reader we took these measurements on, so another team can reproduce the workflow without specialised instrumentation.

### <a id="23d769504e204c439f285a582b15f551" />Best Hardware

<a id="8bbd79cba7db4940b0319a265fc49de5" />Standard URL: [2026.igem.wiki/ubc-vancouver/hardware](https://2026.igem.wiki/ubc-vancouver/hardware)

<a id="7475bed1eca54f0190048599f867286f" />Running a susceptibility test outside a microbiology laboratory takes three devices, and we built all three. The [Microfluidic Chip](/ubc-vancouver/hardware/microfluidic-chip) incubates the patient sample with the candidate antibiotic and delivers the result to the biosensor wells, fabricated from FDM-printed PLA molds for roughly one to five dollars per chip. The [Fluorescence Detector](/ubc-vancouver/hardware/fluorescence-detector) reads the output for \$204.48, against commercial instruments starting around \$630 and reaching beyond \$42,000. The [Bioreactor](/ubc-vancouver/hardware/bioreactor) produces the biosensor cultures for \$106.68 and reached 2.38 to 2.74 times the growth of a flask control across three validation experiments. Our [Best Hardware](/ubc-vancouver/project/best-hardware) page sets out the need, the expert consultations that shaped each device, the functional evidence, and the reproduction materials, including CAD files, engineering drawings, bills of materials, and calibration SOPs.

### <a id="0a1c0fc613484d36abc3dee9fc7fa368" />Best Integrated Human Practices

<a id="6bbf2cf191064519851bafeff0fc2f95" />Standard URL: [2026.igem.wiki/ubc-vancouver/human-practices](https://2026.igem.wiki/ubc-vancouver/human-practices)

<a id="766a3d22e1f84d9fab8d7472d19de20b" />We spoke with nine clinicians, researchers, and patient partners about how resistance affects people locally, and those conversations reached every level of the project. Dr. Hancock's input determined which antibiotics our biosensors detect. Dr. Mróz's input determined how we were permitted to engage patients at all, reframing the work as patient engagement rather than research and producing the consent form that governed it. Sunny Loo's input reached our front-end code, setting font sizes and animation speeds on the patient website we built in response to the isolation that came up in nearly every interview. Our [Best Integrated Human Practices](/ubc-vancouver/project/best-integrated-human-practices) page records what each conversation changed, how the work was integrated across the season, the ethical constraints we placed on ourselves, and what we have not yet established.
