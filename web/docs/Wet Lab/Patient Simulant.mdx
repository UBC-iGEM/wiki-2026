# <a id="3ffd65dd82be80258fdaf0a140f392eb" />Overview

<a id="3fad65dd82be80979d02f971e94b7000" />*E. coli* is one of the most common UTI-causing bacteria in patient populations <Cite refs={[{"n":1,"key":"zhouUrinaryTractInfections2023"}]} />. Resistance to many common and older antibiotics, such as ampicillin, amoxicillin, and tetracycline, is widespread. Resistant bacteria can lead to longer UTI infections and increased treatment costs if the resistant strain is not identified quickly. Therefore, detecting antibiotic resistance in a rapid, sensitive and robust way is a priority.

<a id="3ffd65dd82be80e98520d1a90d5acced" />The FAST microfluidic chip applications requires a patient sample input. To simulate this for assay validation, we need to generate patient simulant strains capable of mimicking antibioitc resistant UTI bacterial samples. Thus, our goals were to:

1. <a id="3fdd65dd82be80c99169dec5570454a4" />Generate bacterial strains with resistance to β-lactams (amoxicillin and cephalexin) and aminoglycosides (kanamycin and gentamicin).
2. <a id="3fdd65dd82be802db54dedc1ee5a2d65" />Confirm the simulant strains’ capacity for antibiotic resistance.
3. <a id="3fdd65dd82be80aebe9dd5e36d4f6992" />Validate and characterize the simulant strains’ antibiotic degradation kinetics.

# <a id="3fad65dd82be80148897d1d1c70afef1" />**Patient Simulant Constructs**

<a id="3fad65dd82be80258a22c564887cb5e6" />We designed three strains using well-characterized antibiotic-resistance genes found on the iGEM registry. This includes a broad-spectrum β-lactamase (BBa\_k1927000), kanamycin-resistance (KanR) cassette (BBa\_P1003), or gentamicin-resistance (GmR) cassette (BBa\_K4235003). Similar to the biosensor reporter constructs, we selected the pSB1C3 backbone as it utilizes chloramphenicol (Cm) resistance for selection. Alternative selectable markers, such as ampicillin or kanamycin resistance, would not be compatible with our intended purpose of modelling antibiotic degradation as they confer resistance to our antibiotics of interest, β-lactams and aminoglycosides.

<a id="3ffd65dd82be803bab34cbe029ece06f" />We followed a standard [Cloning Workflow](/ubc-vancouver/wet-lab/protocols/cloning-workflow) to generate our patient simulant constructs. The pSB1C3 backbone can be linearized with EcoRI and PstI restriction enzymes for downstream HiFi assembly. The patient simulant constructs were generated through 2-fragment gibson assembly reactions in which each fragment was flanked by 20-26 bp overhangs specific to the complementary backbone and insert components. Ultimately, these constructs were transformed into EPI300 *E. coli*, which is a derivative of the non-pathogenic K-12 strain.

# <a id="3fad65dd82be800f81b0e2791204212d" />**Simulant Survival Titration**

<a id="3fad65dd82be80b1afdfd8e6d0a575f2" />In our FAST application, antibiotic resistant patient bacterial samples will degrade or chemically modify their target antibiotics. To confirm that our simulant strains are capable of modelling this interaction, we first identified their minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) ranges. This allows us to identify the upper limit of antibiotic input which enables antibiotic degradation without compromising simulant strain viability. These experiments were a starting point for downstream experiments to validate and quantify antibioitc degradation capacity and kinetics.

<a id="3ffd65dd82be80569484fb4fd9969a00" />We designed [MIC Titration Assay](/ubc-vancouver/wet-lab/protocols/mic-titration-assay) experiments using a two-fold dilution series for gentamicin, tobramycin, cephalexin, and amoxicillin. The β-lactamase simulant strain was tested using β-lactam antibiotics, including cephalexin and amoxicillin. The GmR and KanR simulant strain were tested with their corresponding aminoglycoside antibiotics of gentamicin and kanamycin, respectively. Bacterial growth was assessed after 24 hours of incubation at 37˚C by measuring $OD_{600}$ with a microplate reader. For each iteration of this experiment, we broadened the concentration range to aid in our investigation of MIC ranges for the antibiotic resistant patient simulants. # <a id="3fad65dd82be80e7bcdcc3599d310865" />**Simulant Antibiotic Degradation Validation**

<a id="3ffd65dd82be80b795bbe7b1e62ab6a4" />To investigate the antibiotic degradation facilitated by our patient simulant strains, we designed a 96-well plate [Antibiotic Degradation Assay](/ubc-vancouver/wet-lab/protocols/antibiotic-degradation-assay). We performed these experiments with the goal of determining the minimum exposure time required for antibiotic degradation and quantifying the extent of degradation over time.

## <a id="3ffd65dd82be805a81d9e8e1258b1d78" />Antibiotic Degradation Period

<a id="3ffd65dd82be80bbbf70fd680ea92a6a" />In our FAST microfluidic chip, a patient bacterial sample and an antibiotic of interest are loaded into Chamber A. To model this in our assay, we incubated each patient simulant strain with their corresponding antibiotic at 37˚C for either 6 or 10 hours. The β-lactamase simulant strain was incubated with amoxicillin; the GmR simulant strain was incubated with gentamicin; and the KanR simulant strain was incubated with tobramycin instead of kanamycin to test a more clinically relevant aminoglycoside antibiotic.

<a id="3ffd65dd82be806c8836d49782dd743e" />We tested an initial antibiotic concentration was either 8 µg/ml or 16 µg/ml. This was informed by our [Biosensor Challenge Assay](/ubc-vancouver/wet-lab/lab-notebook/biosensor-challenge-assay), which identified 8 µg/ml as the upper limit of the biosensor MIC range. For the FAST microfluidic chip, in the case that antibiotics are not degraded by the patient bacterial sample, we do not want to exceed the maximum concentration threshold of the biosensor strain in the downstream Chamber B. Secondly, 16 µg/ml was selected since it is twice the upper limit of the biosensor MIC threshold. Patient simulant strains were inoculated at an initial $OD_{600}$ of 0.1.

## <a id="3ffd65dd82be80c5ab65df06c399105a" />Detecting Antibiotic Inactivation

<a id="3fad65dd82be80079c1dc787eb496cf4" />Following the antibiotic degradation period, the cultures were centrifuged and the resulting supernatants were passed through sterile 0.2 µm filters to remove residual simulant bacteria. The supernatant solutions were transferred to a 96 well plate containing the CpxP and RecA biosensor strains in order to detect the presence of active or degraded antibiotics.

<a id="3fad65dd82be80fd9493f71382026678" />After 24 hours of incubation with the biosensor at 37˚C, the $OD_{600}$ was measured as an indicator of bacterial growth or inhibition related to antibiotic activity. These values were then compared with the untreated biosensor growth controls to determine the degree of antibiotic activity remaining after exposure to the patient simulants.

## <a id="3fad65dd82be80128fbdd21d4f3481b1" />Antibiotic Degradation Capacity

<a id="3ffd65dd82be8019949ae3ec2662491d" />The blank-subtracted data was analyzed by calculating Δ$OD_{600}$, normalizing final $OD_{600}$ values to the biosensor growth control wells, which display the baseline growth of the biosensor strains under no antibioitc stress. The interpretation of results may be guided by the following:

- <a id="3ffd65dd82be809ca8e6ca0df956a897" />Δ$OD_{600}$ that is near 0 indicates that significant antibiotic degradation has occurred (i.e. growth is not inhibited such that the final $OD_{600}$ of treated wells is similar to that of the biosensor growth control).
- <a id="3ffd65dd82be8069a768f5094adad424" />Δ$OD_{600}$ that is slightly \< 0 (e.g., between -0.25 and 0) indicates that partial antibiotic degradation has occurred (i.e. growth is only slightly inhibited such that the final $OD_{600}$ of treated wells is slightly lower than that of the the biosensor growth control)
- <a id="3ffd65dd82be802cb471c85e68c9240e" />Δ$OD_{600}$ that is significantly \< 0 indicates that partial antibiotic degradation has occurred (i.e. growth is inhibited such that the final $OD_{600}$ of treated wells is much lower than that of the the biosensor growth control)

<br id="3ffd65dd82be80abaf3ee228c7840405" />

<a id="3fad65dd82be80cea847ea3cedff5707" />Our results indicated that all blank and supernant controls had near-zero $OD_{600}$ values, indicating that there was no media contaminated and that the supernatant filtration successfully removed residual bacterial cells.

<a id="3fad65dd82be80518041e6a47f166a2a" />The results from Figures 1 and 2 indicate that the maximum MIC value of 8 µg/mL was generally a more suitable initial antibiotic concentration for patient simulant-induced degradation in all antibiotics test, compared to the two-fold MIC value of 16 µg/mL. Although, the β-lactamase simulant strain was able to inactivate amoxicillin activity at both concentrations.

<a id="3fad65dd82be80ac8cc9c65a25330a38" />For the β-lactamase simulant strain, exposure to **8 µg/mL of amoxicillin** for **6 hours** resulted in a sufficiently high reduction of amoxicillin activity. At 16 µg/mL, nearly complete amoxicillin degradation was observed, indicated by the near-zero Δ$OD_{600}$ in Figure 1. This was consistent across both the CpxP and RecA biosensors strains.

<a id="3fad65dd82be80fea54ac7d480835d16" />For the KanR simulant strain, exposure to **8 µg/mL of tobramycin** for **6 hours** resulted in sufficiently high antibiotic inactivation, such that Δ$OD_{600}$ did not reach below -0.25. This response was consistent across both the CpxP and RecA biosensors strains.

<a id="3fad65dd82be80548f57d0b7781cfa88" />For the GmR simulant, **8 µg/mL of gentamicin** required the longer exposure condition of **10 hours** to achieve improved degradation. Though, even at a longer exposure duration, its Δ$OD_{600}$is still nearly double that of tobramycin. This response was consistent across both the CpxP and RecA biosensors strains.

<a id="3fad65dd82be80e9a682c3f393c297a8" />Together, these results allowed us to identify suitable conditions for each patient simulant. Our findings for optimal antibiotic input concentration, initial patient simulant $OD_{600}$, and degradation time can be summarized as follows:

<a id="3fad65dd82be8021aa01c9344e7321a0" />

| <a id="3fad65dd82be8091976eee3586256f2b" />Patient simulant strain | Input antibiotic | Initial $OD_{600}$ | Input concentration | Degradation time |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------- | ------------------ | ------------------- | ---------------- |
| <a id="3fad65dd82be809b88c8ca58c8c97531" />GmR simulant            | Gentamicin       | 0.1                | 8 µg/mL             | ≥ 10 hours       |
| <a id="3fad65dd82be805ea3aded0d8682d574" />KanR simulant           | Tobramycin       | 0.1                | 8 µg/mL             | ≤ 6 hours        |
| <a id="3fad65dd82be80119a04e65f2ad5df99" />β-lactamase simulant    | Amoxicillin      | 0.1                | 8–16 µg/mL          | ≤ 6 hours        |

<a id="3fad65dd82be801d9eb5f32bad0d093e" />Although the $OD_{600}$ results indicated reduced antibiotic activity, the fluorescence measurements were lower than in the baseline antibiotic-treated bacteria conditions, suggesting that the degraded antibiotics in the patient-simulant supernatants may affect biosensor GFP fluorescence in our biosensor strains. Therefore, fluorescence and $OD_{600}$ should be interpreted as separate readouts.

# <a id="3fad65dd82be80fd86a8ca20277a7496" />Next Steps

<a id="3ffd65dd82be80db9c9cd7fce8a6152e" />These validation results identify a preliminary range of parameters, which serve as a foundation for future patient simulant degradation assay experiments. Ultimately, the aim is to incorporate our patient simulant strains to validate the FAST microfluidic chip device.
