# <a id="3fcd65dd82be80aba876df2f39145a69" />Cloning

<a id="5c738806410d42d7ae46e471ef9c1223" />

<a id="6f4dfa98ad034aefafad4fa433e17ed5" />

<Dbtl>
  <Fragment slot="design">
    <a id="3e3079297ea14afdb2d391fe7fccc0ce" />Based on our literature review, selected two stress response promoters to detect the presence of active antibiotics. This informed our design of two fluorescent reporter constructs. The CpxP reporter construct (2940 bp) uses the *cpxP* promoter, which responds to cell envelope stress. The RecA reporter construct (2855 bp) uses the *recA* promoter and its SOS box, which respond to DNA damage.

    <a id="247272b6bbe04ee098f310ef4c62111b" />Both reporters are generated using the pSB1C3 backbone for 3 reasons:

    - <a id="aef5dd50a3b44cfcb8cbfd813507fdd4" />Host compatibility: it carries a pMB1 origin of replication which works in *E. coli*.
    - <a id="e43acb2680ed4d60ba5fbc7fa2a4de4e" />Copy number: pMB1 is a high-copy origin, which increases signal strength for an sfGFP reporter.
    - <a id="f4cc1fac8d4f44f68900f6f2955d06e6" />Selection marker: it carries chloramphenicol resistance (CmR). We avoided AmpR and KanR backbones because those resistance genes would affect our modelling of β-lactam and aminoglycoside induced stress.
  </Fragment>

  <Fragment slot="build">
    <a id="ed18330470794297ac441a7cac21d7b5" />For assembly, we ordered gBlocks gene fragments from IDT with Gibson overhangs built in:

    - <a id="b898b792f83f43fb984d2916c5ac8add" />Two linearized backbone fragments (2079 bp), each with overhangs specific to one construct.
    - <a id="96955b79efb2422ea15429a026b08cc2" />A CpxP reporter insert (947 bp) containing our $\mathrm{P}_\textit{cpxP}$-*sfGFP* reporter part ([BBa\_26A5JL4T](https://registry.igem.org/parts/bba-26a5jl4t)) and overhangs specific to the regions flanking the pSB1C3 backbone MCS
    - <a id="bea0c3ddd4a3419faea755b8062141ed" />A RecA reporter insert (862 bp) containing our $\mathrm{P}_\textit{recA}$-*sfGFP* reporter part ([BBa\_267O4B7H](https://registry.igem.org/parts/bba-267o4b7h)) with overhangs specific to the regions flanking the pSB1C3 backbone MCS

    <a id="b3ebc4864a9a4ecd8ecedd1958297ab2" />We assembled each reporter by Gibson assembly, combining the linearized backbone with the corresponding insert. We transformed the assemblies into competent *E. coli* DH5α and selected colonies grown on chloramphenicol plates.
  </Fragment>

  <Fragment slot="test">
    <a id="dab5a5b5230f43f1a9c0700fa2036e29" />We extracted plasmids from colonies of each construct and sequenced them to confirm successful cloning.
  </Fragment>

  <Fragment slot="learn">
    <a id="0955a7efdc9044b08a0462392991cf1a" />Sequencing confirmed that both reporters are assembled as designed. While some point mutations were identified, their impact was negligible. As such, we proceeded to transform our constructs into EPI300 *E. coli*.
  </Fragment>
</Dbtl>

# <a id="3fcd65dd82be8007a578d37cd26249d0" />Characterization

## <a id="3fcd65dd82be80ff8da3c4247b714f89" />Growth Curve

<a id="5585c7480243489e973985f1f358fd31" />

<a id="32c1510ad8714c33b39ca00c8d0fb372" />

<Dbtl>
  <Fragment slot="design">
    <a id="c13c27e2a1284a868283c3db8dd7e580" />Before validating our reporter strains, we needed to first generate a curve that measures the baseline growth kinetics of the WT EPI300 strain compared to the biosensor strains. This is helpful for determining the metabolic burden of the plasmid backbone as well as baseline GFP expression.

    <a id="f87612f37e124c9985b6493749f1bdae" />Additionally, for our microfluidic chip application, we needed to characterize bacterial growth and baseline GFP expression at ambient temperature compared to 37˚C.
  </Fragment>

  <Fragment slot="build">
    <a id="91dd3f3d8a814b3c8c2b3669151990b5" />We adapted a growth curve protocol for an automated workflow using the BMG PHERAstar FSX microplate reader to measure $OD_{600}$ and sfGFP fluorescence (485 nm excitation, 510 nm emission).

    <a id="0fa5096263e94a038932e490959d40b8" />We considered all necessary control conditions to elucidate metabolic burden, including a WT EPI300 strain for baseline growth kinetics, EPI300 transformed with the reporter backbone to determine metabolic burden of the plasmid, as well as the RecA and CpxP biosensor strains to determine the impact of baseline GFP expression on growth.
  </Fragment>

  <Fragment slot="test">
    <a id="e3c8fd22aece4c31a16aaa1ab27c637e" />We measured $OD_{600}$ and fluorescence intensity throughout a 24-hour timeframe. For the first 12 hours, we took measurements every 30 minutes for the curve generated at 37˚C and every 60 minutes for the curve generated at ambient temperature. For the last 12 hours, we took measurements every 60 minutes for both curves.
  </Fragment>

  <Fragment slot="learn">
    <a id="0e6c35baa16d4190b3c00df0430711d8" />We observed that introducing the backbone decreases the growth maximum relative to WT EPI300. However, biosensor growth remains similar at 37˚C and 22˚C. Additionally, we observed basal GFP expression in the CpxP biosensor strain. However, this basal  expression ceases at 22˚C.

    <a id="954ef51b00f3485f98c5dd8df6f550f6" />Overall, the generated growth curve will serve as a standard to normalize our fluorescent readouts in downstream reporter validation assays, accounting for the impact of bacterial density on GFP expression.
  </Fragment>
</Dbtl>

# <a id="3fcd65dd82be803397e6c89a77ff59f8" />Reporter Validation

## <a id="3fed65dd82be804f8b3ff9a17630a54b" />Antibiotic-Induced Fluorescence Output

<details>
  <summary>
    ### <a id="3fcd65dd82be807da888f6d15162c5eb" />Iteration 1 [Biosensor GFP sensitivity assay v1 ](/ubc-vancouver/wet-lab/lab-notebook/biosensor-gfp-sensitivity-assay-v1-)
  </summary>

  <a id="0e9421de46024a32806b4eabba48c26d" />

  <a id="e006de7fb4104f9ba6f228f013118061" />

  <Dbtl>
    <Fragment slot="design">
      <a id="60cb4f3760314c00ad59e20e0b0361dd" />After transformation, we needed to validate our construct’s biological function as a sensor. To this end, we started by looking for differential expression of green fluorescent protein (GFP) to determine a range of subinhibitory antibiotic concentrations to use, as well its incubation time. We also considered the effect of optical density ($OD_{600}$) on sfGFP expression, and included this as a variable in the experiment.
    </Fragment>

    <Fragment slot="build">
      <a id="cc804a6348104eeab4e92eaa845166fb" />RecA and CpxP biosensor strains were inoculated and harvested, then diluted to 1x at $OD_{600}$ of 0.4. Antibiotics (Amoxicillin, Kanamycin, Gentamicin, Cephalexin) were reconstituted, and serially diluted twice. In a clear, U-bottom 96-well plate, LB-Cm media was added into the wells for antibiotic conditions, then given the associated serial dilution of antibiotic. This combination was diluted further using the biosensor strains at an equal volume. Wells were allotted for blank LB-Cm and unstimulated biosensor strain controls. Each condition was done in triplicate, including controls.
    </Fragment>

    <Fragment slot="test">
      <a id="03762d27804746459e200a6591979131" />We evaluated the plate using a microplate reader machine, exciting at 488 nm and reading fluorescence at 510 nm. The plate was incubated at 37˚C, and scanned every 10 minutes over one hour.
    </Fragment>

    <Fragment slot="learn">
      <a id="33e71c313c104545856f39f6b1c4c50f" />We had four major findings:

      1. <a id="2b8a92c2576e4345be36b97e8f677b7c" />The RecA biosensor appeared more sensitive, with significantly higher fold changes observed in the same timescale
      2. <a id="2bbde597ff684963ac3fba1ca8c6bf53" />Higher $OD_{600}$ values resulted in more discernible GFP signals
      3. <a id="24c05adb39b746ac9904b076cdb9bce0" />Most conditions had a peak between 10-30 minutes
      4. <a id="bec5d327d57a468484ca50efc9e4c170" />We identified potential antibiotic concentration and time value ranges for optimal GFP-stimulation response in both CpxP and RecA, although RecA showed more promise.

      <a id="035e3c3598c045789120a02adc73cbf8" />However, this experiment had some setbacks, like the U-bottom plates not being optimal for plate reading, as well as low overall expression, $OD_{600}$, and identified ranges.
    </Fragment>
  </Dbtl>
</details>

  <Dbtl>
    <Fragment slot="design">
      <a id="53e00fa12b0c4e2a97884e43f69d4555" />In the previous experiment, we identified potential ranges for antibiotic concentrations and treatment times. However, these ranges were narrow, and we did not adequately assess the lower and upper limits of the GFP stimulation response, as changes in response across different antibiotic concentrations were minimal. Additionally, the cell density (by way of $OD_{600}$) was low, and our findings in the previous experiment show that $OD_{600}$ values increased with sfGFP output. As such, we elected to test one condition this time for simplicity. Finally, the results from this experiment were intended to inform a full factorial design experiment that could characterize our biosensor with greater resolution than manual work.
    </Fragment>

    <Fragment slot="build">
      <a id="6ef62c29d30d4ccea54faaa5011ea98a" />We learned in the last experiment that the $OD_{600}$ was too low, so the biosensor strains were grown to an $OD_{600}$ of 0.8 instead of 0.4. Reconstituted antibiotics (Amoxicillin, Kanamycin, Gentamicin, Cephalexin) were serially diluted five times instead of twice, with the exact concentrations depending on the antibiotic.

      <a id="18ce26850dad4b54a68ef7fcaa5c61ce" />In an opaque, flat-bottom, 96-well plate, LB-Cm media was pipetted into the wells allotted for antibiotic conditions, then given the associated antibiotic dilutions at 2x concentration. These were diluted by an identical volume of the associated biosensor strain at $OD_{600}$ of 0.8, to a final $OD_{600}$ of 0.4 and antibiotic concentration of 1x.
    </Fragment>

    <Fragment slot="test">
      <a id="35b1dc9d7f1141e88ad4f56ad01b31b5" />We evaluated the plate using a microplate reader, exciting at 488 nm and reading fluorescence at 510 nm. The plate was incubated at 37˚C, and scanned every 10 minutes over the span of 40 minutes.
    </Fragment>

    <Fragment slot="learn">
      <a id="35c906cc8f9e44fd8d5df4f7ca0520ae" />We encountered significant technical issues, including microplate reader plate jamming, which led to loss of time and data. Nonetheless, we identified preliminary ranges for further exploration.
    </Fragment>
  </Dbtl>

  <Dbtl>
    <Fragment slot="design">
      <a id="99ca6700ed6a44d2a54898ebfc582402" />The previous iteration identified preliminary fluorescence peaks under some antibiotic conditions, but instrument errors and inconsistent measurements prevented us from defining reliable concentration ranges. We therefore repeated the broad screen to identify the concentration bounds for a future factorial design experiment.
    </Fragment>

    <Fragment slot="build">
      <a id="c40897b610b040e190b2d3cfe88ce1a1" />CpxP and RecA were exposed to 13 two-fold serial dilutions of gentamicin, kanamycin, amoxicillin, cephalexin ranging from 0.0156-320 µg/mL, each condition tested in triplicate. The cultures were mixed 1:1 with antibiotic containing media alongside untreated controls, a backbone control, and media blanks. Because cultures were plated before reaching the intended density, the final starting $OD_{600}$ was 0.2 rather than 0.4. Fluorescence and $OD_{600}$ measurements were collected every 10 minutes over the 50-minute assay.
    </Fragment>

    <Fragment slot="test">
      <a id="6cabca0528e54342a97ff27bbfd9aac7" />Fluorescence changed minimally across the tested conditions and remained close to the media control, making fold-change calculations sensitive to variations in background signal. Unusually low $OD_{600}$ measurements affected an entire plate column at a single time point, suggesting a measurement artifact rather. $OD_{600}$ generally did not decrease following antibiotic exposure and in some cases increased, although these measurements alone could not determine between bacterial growth or increased turbidity.
    </Fragment>

    <Fragment slot="learn">
      <a id="4eee606ccee2482fbc9f764cfae53b57" />Overall this iteration did not establish reproducible antibiotic concentration ranges identified previously. Interpretation was limited by fluorescence values close to background, inconsistent measurements, and a lower starting bacterial density than originally planned. Rather than selecting concentrations from uncertain peaks in this broad screen, the next iteration used independently determined MIC ranges and a longer assay to investigate responses that may not be detectable during a short assay.
    </Fragment>
  </Dbtl>

    ### <a id="3fcd65dd82be80cc9855d03259338059" />Iteration 4 [Biosensor Response Titration v1](/ubc-vancouver/wet-lab/lab-notebook/biosensor-response-titration-v1)

  <a id="ce482984b3e743ad9184fbcd9e8f6775" />

  <Dbtl>
    <Fragment slot="design">
      <a id="c4901050d8394c89bd46f7d76109e50e" />Because broad screening did not establish a reproducible concentration-dependent fluorescence, we used the results from the Biosensor Challenge Assay to focus our next experiment. Both biosensors had estimated MICs between 4 µg/mL to 8 µg/mL for tobramycin, amoxicillin, and gentamicin., so we tested concentrations at our below this range and extended the observation period to eight hours.
    </Fragment>

    <Fragment slot="build">
      <a id="09e7f8666f154785841375e253b59c12" />CpxP and RecA were exposed to 9 two-fold serial dilutions of gentamicin, tobramycin, or amoxicillin, each condition tested in triplicate. The cultures were mixed 1:1 with antibiotic-containing media to a final starting $OD_{600}$ of 0.1, alongside untreated biosensor controls, a backbone control, and media blanks. $OD_{600}$ and fluorescence were measured from 0.5 to 8 hours.
    </Fragment>

    <Fragment slot="test">
      <a id="cfc87a5735464069b183c7d9526d662d" />CpxP showed dose-dependent fluorescence responses at later time points. At the highest  amoxicillin concentration, fluorescence was approximately two- to three-fold higher than the untreated control between four- to eight-hours. Gentamicin and tobramycin, fluorescence peaked at the four-hour interval for the highest tested concentration condition.

      <a id="0b0adea919d841f0a0324a97a05b16c5" />RecA showed a different pattern. Increase fluorescence associated with amoxicillin concentration was observed at four to eight hours, particularly at the highest tested concentration. However, the aminoglycosides, gentamicin and tobramycin, did not produce a dose-dependent response. $OD_{600}$ also increased over the assay, indicating that difference in bacterial growth could contribute to total fluorescence.
    </Fragment>

    <Fragment slot="learn">
      <a id="c6c383efe66949aeaba41e186fdafe78" />Extending this assay revealed responses at four to eight hours that were not clear in the short-duration screens, although these responses were longer than desired for a rapid assay. Reporter performance also strongly depended on the antibiotic identity and the earlier conclusion from iteration 1 that RecA was the more sensitive reporter was not supported. The later timepoints were therefore identified as candidates for future testing before moving to a factorial design experiment.
    </Fragment>
  </Dbtl>
