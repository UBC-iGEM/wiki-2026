# Purpose

To generate our biosensor strain, we will transform our two reporters (recA and cpxP) into non-pathogenic K12 derivative *E. coli*, EPI300.

# Materials & Methods

<span underline="true">Materials</span>

- Chloramphenicol LB agar plate (17 mg/L Cm concentration)
- EPI300 (stocks provided by Hallam Lab)
- Cloned reporters (recA and CpxP) from [Notebook Entry 4](/wet-lab/lab-notebook-2026/notebook-entry-4)
- NEB SOC Outgrowth Medium
- 42°C water bath

<span underline="true">Methods</span>

1. <span discussion-urls="discussion\://3a0d65dd-82be-8006-a207-f9238485ada8/3a0d65dd-82be-80f7-b7e2-f2c2756147c9/3a0d65dd-82be-805c-b8fd-001c010d9379">Mix </span>45 µL of <span discussion-urls="discussion\://3a0d65dd-82be-8006-a207-f9238485ada8/3a0d65dd-82be-80f7-b7e2-f2c2756147c9/3a0d65dd-82be-805c-b8fd-001c010d9379">EPI300</span> stock with 5 µL of cloned plasmid (recA or CpxP)
   1. pipette up and down to mix
2. Incubate on ice for 30 minutes
3. Heat shock at 42°C for 30 seconds
4. Incubate on ice for 5 minutes
5. Add 100 µL\*\* \*\*of SOC outgrowth medium and incubate at 37°C for 1 hour
6. Plate 50 uL on each plate and incubate at 37°C overnight

# Summary

Colony growth on selective plates demonstrates the successful transformation of CmR-containing plasmids into EPI300. These biosensor strains will be saved in glycerol stocks and used for subsequent validation experiments.
