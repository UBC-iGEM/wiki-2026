# <a id="3ffd65dd82be80a5a92ef05d3a5aaab0" />Overview

<a id="3fdd65dd82be80359477e04d64399077" />Our goal was to develop biosensor strains capable of detecting the presence of antibiotics through stress-responsive reporter systems that generate a fluorescent signal. Specifically, our experiments generated and characterized the CpxP and RecA biosensors, as well as produced $OD_{600}$ calibration curves to normalize fluorescent measurements from our biosensor readouts.

<a id="3fdd65dd82be80c79ecfd36364e544c1" />To achieve these objectives, we transformed EPI300 *E. coli* with the CpxP and RecA reporter constructs. To characterize the growth kinetics and baseline superfolder green fluorescent protein (sfGFP) expression of our engineered strains, we performed a growth curve experiment. Subsequently, to determine a subinhibitory range of antibiotics for our biosensors, we performed a minimal inhibitory concentrations (MIC) assay. These results narrowed down an optimal antibiotic concentration range to validate the the sfGFP expression sensitivity of our biosensor strains.

# <a id="3fad65dd82be802e8b2ae06e4c514213" />**Biosensor Constructs**

<a id="3fdd65dd82be80a78313caf68bfa742c" />Both reporters utilized the pSB1C3 backbone, which confers chloramphenicol (Cm) resistance for selection. Alternative selectable markers, such as ampicillin or kanamycin resistance, would not be compatible with our intended applications as they confer resistance to β-lactam and aminoglycoside antibiotics, respectively.

<a id="3ffd65dd82be8016a066ebe78e209367" />To generate our biosensor constructs, we followed a standard [Cloning Workflow](/ubc-vancouver/wet-lab/protocols/cloning-workflow). To linearize this backbone for the insertion of our reporter [Parts](/ubc-vancouver/wet-lab/parts), EcoRI and PstI restriction enzymes can be used to enable downstream HiFi assembly. The reporter constructs were generated through 2-fragment Gibson assemblies in which each fragment was flanked by 20-26 bp overhangs specific to the complementary backbone and insert components. Ultimately, these constructs express sfGFP when the cell experiences antibioitc-related stress, particularly cell wall stress with the CpxP reporter construct and DNA damage with the RecA reporter construct. The fast maturing sfGFP protein folds within 13.6 minutes, allowing for rapid fluorescence output detection in our FAST microfluidic chip application <Cite refs={[{"n":1,"key":"lambertSuperfolderGFPFPbasea"}]} />. Cloning success was confirmed through sequencing. Subsequently, the final reporter constructs were transformed into EPI300 *E. coli*, which is a derivative of the non-pathogenic K-12 strain.

# <a id="3fad65dd82be80178e5af6c8962dca2f" />Characterization

## <a id="3fad65dd82be8083ab32c2c0a5db3547" />**Growth Curve Experiment**

<a id="3fdd65dd82be80589f80f638849e334a" />To establish benchmarking performance of our biosensor strains relative to wild type EPI300 *E. coli*, developed an automated approach to generate [Bacterial Growth Curves](/ubc-vancouver/wet-lab/protocols/bacterial-growth-curves). These growth curves were obtained by measuring optical density at 600 nm ($OD_{600}$) in the absence of β-lactam or aminoglycoside antibiotics. Simultaneously, to determine baseline sfGFP expression, we measured fluorescence intensity at 485 nm (excitation) and 510 nm (emission) for each growth curve time point.

<a id="3fdd65dd82be802a903ccaaab0ac8ad6" />Measurements of $OD_{600}$ reveal that the maximum cell densities of the biosensor strains were approximately 35% lower than that of the WT strain. This suggests that the plasmids may impose a metabolic burden on the transformed strains. However, there is minimal difference in biosensor growth maximums at 22˚C compared to 37˚C. Ultimately, this growth curve can be used to normalize fluorescence readouts, to account for the impact of cell density on sfGFP expression.

<a id="3fdd65dd82be80ddaeadd9d647fb675e" />Spectrometric measurements revealed substantially higher basal sfGFP expression in the CpxP biosensor strain compared with both the WT EPI300 and RecA biosensor strains, reaching approximately 18–20-fold higher levels after 25 hours. However, this basal sfGFP expression ceases at 22˚C.

## <a id="3fad65dd82be8079befbc4c2621d5550" />**Plasmid Retention Experiment**

<a id="3fad65dd82be8078986aceb67f09dcd2" />To determine whether chloramphenicol (Cm) antibiotic selection remains stable during the biosensor experiments, we tested how long the CpxP and RecA strains could retain their reporter plasmids after transfer into Cm-free LB. Removing Cm would be useful for removing the confounding effect that it has on the bacterial stress response, but requiring that the biosensor plasmids remained stable without selection.

<a id="3fdd65dd82be80dcb28ee7850aaeb746" />We designed an agar plate based [Plasmid Retention Assay](/ubc-vancouver/wet-lab/protocols/plasmid-retention-assay). To test plasmid retention over time, CpxP and RecA biosensor cultures were grown in LB media with or without Cm for up to two hours, then plated on LB-Cm agar plates. Since the reporter plasmids confer Cm resistance, the growth of colonies on LB-Cm plates would indicate that the plasmid is retented. The WT EPI300 served as a negative control and the biosensor strains cultured in LB-Cm served as a positive control for reporter plasmid retention.

<a id="3fdd65dd82be803e9904e4dd89054d4e" />As expected, biosensor strains cultured in LB-Cm grew on LB-Cm plates while WT EPI300 cultured in either LB or LB-Cm did not. In contrast, biosensors cultured in Cm-free LB showed no detectable growth after one hour, indicating the rapid loss of their plasmid. These results demonstrated that Cm selection was necessary to maintain the biosensor construct during the experiment, despite its potential influence on the bacterial stress response. As such, all our downstream biosensor experiments employed LB-Cm.

# <a id="3fad65dd82be80c5a420c96e1b62a059" />**Validation**

## <a id="3fad65dd82be80ffbc1cc433e0f4077f" />**Antibiotic-Induced Fluorescence Output Experiments**

<a id="3ffd65dd82be80a58ee1cc738d87fe53" />In our biosensors strains, sfGFP fluorescence intensity serves as the output to quantify antibiotic-related stress promoter activation, indirectly detecting the presence of active antibiotics. As such, we performed validation experiments to confirm that our biosensor strains are capable of producing a distinguishable fluorescent signal relative to the unstimulated controls, which produce minimal baseline levels of sfGFP in the absence antibiotics. These experiments were designed to titrate our antibiotics of interest in order to determine the determine the optimal antibiotic concentration that produces the most responsive sfGFP expression.

## <a id="3ffd65dd82be809797dfd219f663d7e0" />Initial Fluorescence Sensitivity Screening Titrations

<a id="3ffd65dd82be801fa601cd4327b0500b" />Our initial validation experiments screened a wide range of antibiotic concentrations to determine a feasible range at which the CpxP and RecA biosensor strains produced a detectable fluorescent response. This was achieved through a two-fold serial dilution, testing 13 concentrations per antibiotic.

While these experiments did not reveal a dose-dependent fluorescence output response, they suggested that our initial 60-minute window was not sufficient to generate differential sfGFP expression.

## <a id="3ffd65dd82be801ca600f2dac56c1d37" />**Overnight Biosensor Strain MIC Titration**

<a id="3ffd65dd82be800694cfc8e9c6043821" />To troubleshoot the challenges of generating a distinguishable dose-dependent fluorescent signal, we expanded our antibiotic exposure time and modified our concentration ranges. Before continuing to validate biosensor fluorescence output sensitivity, we first needed to identify the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) ranges for our biosensor strains. This allows us to narrow down a subinhibitory range of antibiotic concentrations where the biosensor strains remain viable while still experiencing antibiotic-related stress.

<a id="3ffd65dd82be8056b658c52e76ac03af" />To determine the MIC ranges of the CpxP and RecA biosensors, we performed a [MIC Titration Assay](/ubc-vancouver/wet-lab/protocols/mic-titration-assay) using two-fold serial dilutions for gentamicin, tobramycin, and amoxicillin. # <a id="3ffd65dd82be80bdac24e984fcf40be7" />Applications in FAST

<a id="3ffd65dd82be808f8bfff5090da5867a" />Ultimately, the CpxP and RecA biosensors will be implemented into the FAST microfluidic chip device to provide a rapid fluorescent readout of β-lactam and aminoglycoside susceptibility.

## <a id="3ffd65dd82be80e1a72dd8c24a46b82a" />Detecting Antibiotic Susceptibility in Patient Samples

<a id="3ffd65dd82be8037a359f9632187bc07" />In the FAST microfluidic chip, a patient sample and antibiotic of interest (e.g., 8 µg/mL of amoxicillin) are loaded into Chamber A. If the patient sample is susceptible, the active antibiotic will enter Chamber B where it meets either the CpxP or RecA biosensor strain. Depending on the input antibiotic, this interaction triggers the Cpx cell wall stress response or SOS DNA damage pathway. This subsequently activates the *cpxP* promoter or *recA* promoter, respectively, to induce the expression of sfGFP. The fluorescent output indicates that the patient sample is susceptible to the antibiotic input.

## <a id="3ffd65dd82be806c8e0aecc0f4855281" />Detecting Antibiotic Resistance in Patient Samples

<a id="3ffd65dd82be80f8b2daf2ef7b610b64" />Similarly, a patient sample and antibiotic of interest (e.g., 8 µg/mL of amoxicillin) are loaded into Chamber A. If the patient sample is resistant, antibiotic degradation or modification will occur and inactivated antibiotics will enter Chamber B and meet the either the CpxP or RecA biosensor. This interaction will not sufficiently trigger the Cpx cell wall stress response or SOS DNA damage pathway. As such, there is minimal stress promoter induced expression of sfGFP, leading to a negligible fluorescent readout. This indicates that the patient sample is resistant to the antibiotic input.

# <a id="3ffd65dd82be80eb9950e6764b425b20" />**Future Directions**

<a id="3ffd65dd82be8053addac9979551d2c3" />The overall aim was to generate the CpxP and RecA biosensor strains and validate whether their fluorescence outputs could serve as meaningful indicators of bacterial stress responses to antibiotics. Our characterization and validation of these biosensor strains established the experimental parameters and limitations of our reporter systems. These preliminary results will inform future experiments using a factorial design approach to further narrow down optimal parameters by systematically testing smaller increments of antibiotic concentrations and fluorescence measurement time points. Ultimately, these findings provide a foundation for future optimization towards our rapid antibiotic susceptibility testing platform.
