Abstract
As our project this year was to develop an E. coli promoter-GFP biosensor, we sought to identify high quality promoters—characterized by dynamic range, low basal expression, and low cross-activation—that enable quantifying presence of antibiotic in solution. We wanted to capture the response to four anitibiotics: Amoxicillin, Cephalexin, Gentamicin, and Tobramycin. \*\*Method
The objective of the Promoter Library was to identify top promoter candidates out of a library of over 200 promoter candidates. The library consisted of promoter-GFP plates. Our experiment consisted of two parts: an assay development to select sub-inhibitory concentrations for each antibiotic, and the high-throughput library screen exposing the promoter-GFP plates to the antibiotics at the selected concentrations. As we had many promoter candidates, doing this experiment on our own bench top would not be effective. Thus, we collaborated with Biofactorial, a High-Throughput Biology and Automation Facility here at UBC’s Life Sciences Institute. Biofactorial was able to execute the testing for us, so we worked in collaboration to develop the proposal and experimental design.

Our criteria for evaluating top promoter candidates is as follows:

|                  |                                       |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Criterion        | Requirement                           |
| Basal Expression | Low and stable in untreated cultures  |
| Fold Induction   | At least two-fold during screening    |
| Absolute Output  | Clearly above promoterless background |
