#### **Abstract**

Rapid detection of antibiotic resistance requires predicting whether a resistance enzyme can degrade a target antibiotic quickly enough to be diagnostically distinguishable from a susceptible strain. To stimulate the enzymatic degradation of antibiotics within a microfluidic chip in a well-mixed environment, we developed a non-spatial ordinary differential equation  model coupling three biological processes. Each are represented by their own kinetic framework: enzyme induction, antibiotic degradation and bacterial kill/regrowth. The model integrates Hill kinetics, Michaelis-Menten kinetics, and a sigmoidal Emax framework, parameterized where possible by using published enzymatic, kinetic, and phrarmodynamic data. The model provides a visualization of three coupled state variables over time: enzyme concentration (E), antibiotic concentration (A), and bacterial population (N).
The purpose of this model is to predict the time required for antibiotic concentration to fall below defined fractions of a clinically relevant starting thresholds such as (1x MIC), and provide a quantitive basis for Wet Lab in evaluating assay feasibility and guiding experimental design.
