## <a id="3fcd65dd82be8018b984cd9454040b94" />Abstract

<a id="3fcd65dd82be80abace3f8c39492880e" />Bacterial biosensors have potential applications in detecting biological relevant signals, but their performance may be influenced by the chemical and biological conditions of the environment in which they operate. In our project, an E. coli promoter-GFP biosensor is used without insight to how the biological systems can change the output.

<a id="3fcd65dd82be808db2dcecc17e9642c6" />This study therefore aims to investigate the performance of the E. coli promoter-GFP biosensor under simulated patient-infection conditions and characterize factors that may influence their response including antibiotic concentration, bacterial resistance backgrounds, and environmental conditions such as sodium and glucose concentrations. Using a Design of Experiments approach n collaboration with Biofactorial, an automation facility,  we systematically evaluate biosensor responses across selected experimental conditions.

## <a id="3fcd65dd82be803a8955e012f9b78687" />Method

<a id="3fcd65dd82be808697e9d3cdfd6317ad" />The objective of the Patient Sensor Design of Experiments (DOE) was to characterize the performance of the bacterial sensors by varying biological and chemical conditions and analyzing the fluorescence data.

#### <a id="3fcd65dd82be8025b37ac129a28557f5" />Experimental Design

<a id="3fcd65dd82be80c29675e2c88c25ae30" />A reduced DOE approach (using Box Behnken design) was developed using JMP in collaboration with Biofactorial to investigate the effects of bacterial and environmental conditions on the promoter-GFP biosensor fluorescence output. This approach was chosen over a full factorial design to reduce the number of experimental conditions while allowing the evaluation of multiple factors.

<a id="3fcd65dd82be80c39299f02761561fde" />The study examined the E. coli biosensors containing //TODO, which were evaluated under different simulated patient-infection conditions.

<a id="3fcd65dd82be8042a8c6edbb57e6f370" />Experimental conditions were established using two liquid-handling systems. The WellMate delivered bacterial cultures and media, while the Echo dispensed chemical treatments. Fluorescence was measured as the reporter readout to assess biosensor responses across the tested conditions.

#### <a id="3fcd65dd82be80ce8084d735fed8e6ea" />Biological Factors -Wellmate Delivery

<a id="3fcd65dd82be80b08b21d4d6054e7d1c" />The biological factors included the biosensor promoter, culture medium, and patient-infection condition. The patient-infection conditions included a no-infection control, untransformed E. coli EPI300, and bacterial cultures representing different antibiotic-resistance backgrounds: β-lactamase, gentamicin resistance (GMR), and kanamycin resistance (KanR). LB and LB-Cm media were used as applicable to the corresponding cultures.

<br id="3fcd65dd82be80abad72c4f8aea748d7" />

#### <a id="3fcd65dd82be8082aaf0cac878ac4230" />Chemical Factors -Echo delivery

<a id="3fcd65dd82be80c5a79bed7909d0b80f" />The chemical factors were introduced using the Echo liquid-handling system. Antibiotic was used as a factor, and was varied as a categorical condition, either with no antibiotic or varying concentrations. Amoxicillin, gentamicin, and tobramycin were the antibiotics used. Sodium and glucose were also used as chemical variables, each with three planned concentration levels.
