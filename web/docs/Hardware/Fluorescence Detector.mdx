# <a id="3f9d65dd82be8075b6b2f8a74de1c8fd" />Principles of Fluorescence

<a id="3f9d65dd82be8050867be53a9a577c19" />Fluorescence is critical to many experiments and research applications, such as flow cytometry, fluorescence immunoassays, and more. Fluorescence helps us identify cellular structures, quantify colonies, and even detect cell susceptibility to antibiotics.

<a id="3fad65dd82be80059b4bf8b57504d9bd" />Cells fluoresce when a fluorophore absorbs light energy (a photon), causing its electrons to transition from the ground state (s0) to an excited state (s1). As some energy is lost during this process, the emitted photon carries less energy and therefore has a longer wavelength, as shown in Figure 1. This phenomenon is known as the Stokes shift <Cite refs={[{"n":1,"key":"sandersonFluorescenceMicroscopy2014"}]} />  This indicates that when blue light is absorbed, green light of lower energy may be emitted, producing fluorescence.

# <a id="3f9d65dd82be8054b0a7da2a0caffc89" />Motivation for Fluorescence Detector

<a id="3fcd65dd82be80ef9515d6d95b2206a7" />Our microfluidic chips primarily utilize fluorescence to provide binary information on antibiotic resistance, enabling rapid preliminary screening before further assessments are conducted. However, we realized that detecting fluorescence still requires equipment that may be difficult to access in hospital settings, contradicting our goal of rapid, point-of-care detection. To address this limitation, we further refined our design through iterative development and brainstorming, eventually deciding to create a complementary fluorescence detector.

## <a id="3f9d65dd82be80b4bb54d4d68e2b0c45" />Conventional vs. Our Fluorescence Detector

<a id="3fad65dd82be8060b66ec7bc55f0ed97" />To identify the limitations of existing fluorescence detectors, we conducted preliminary market research. Based on our findings, fluorescence detection equipment can range in price from hundreds of dollars (i.e. \$630.39 <Cite refs={[{"n":4,"key":"DarkBoxUV"}]} /> ) to tens of thousands of dollars (i.e. \$42191 <Cite refs={[{"n":5,"key":"ltdFluorescenceSpectrophotometerNFS100"}]} /> ). Such costs make it financially impractical to equip every clinician with a fluorescence detector used only for preliminary screening. Furthermore, although both handheld and benchtop detectors are available on the market, their designs are not compatible with our microfluidic chips.

<a id="3fcd65dd82be80868e51fbc00a29dc5b" />To improve the accessibility of our point-of-care detection system, we incorporated several design features into our customized fluorescence detector: a holder tailored to our microfluidic chip, a user-friendly interface, a simplified fluorescence detection circuit to reduce material costs, and an innovative component assembly method that ensures both effective light shielding and ease of use.

<a id="3fcd65dd82be80519f6edb341fb10bad" />With our customized fluorescence detector, we aim to make antibiotic resistance screening accessible, fast, and affordable.

# <a id="3fbd65dd82be80beb3d8f05eb568492f" />What’s New About Our Design

## <a id="3fad65dd82be8089b32fdc7e914f0a6a" />Snap-and-Seal Enclosure

<a id="3fdd65dd82be80ed88efe69ee7e79de6" />Before beginning the design of our fluorescence detector, we researched previous iGEM projects to explore existing approaches and achievements.

<a id="3fdd65dd82be806d9421fc5bc19b0502" />Team Munich’s 2017 design inspired us with its compact fluorescence detector, which used magnets to sandwich the detection components between 3D-printed parts <Cite refs={[{"n":6,"key":"TeamMunich2017igemorg"}]} />. This approach demonstrated the potential of magnets to improve user-friendliness and provide a convenient, efficient assembly method.

<a id="3fed65dd82be80a8a6bdca626dbf5c1e" />As demonstrated in the 3D model, the detector is divided into two halves: Part A and Part B (label). Part A serves as the base, housing a sliding track for the microfluidic holder and the circuit components responsible for fluorescence detection. Part B sits on top of Part A to form a completely dark enclosure. It also houses the illumination system and user interface for active control over the on and off of the light.

<a id="3fed65dd82be803da560c12777722089" />So, how exactly do our magnets improve the design's functionality?

<a id="3fed65dd82be809d8589f767f9e2b9c8" />**1. Open, lay flat, and access with ease**

<a id="3fed65dd82be80cd9a94f69c71cb51dc" />The two halves can be separated and laid completely flat on your workspace, allowing you to:

- <a id="3fed65dd82be80efb0f0f09b77c0ffb6" />Load the microfluidics with ease.
- <a id="3fed65dd82be80f7a02cda04a4ab29ae" />Freely interact with the integrated switch and other user-interface components without having to maneuver in a tight space.
- <a id="3fed65dd82be8087b344d76601de356d" />Clean and maintain the device with minimal hassle.

<a id="3fed65dd82be80e0aa56fa81ee7a3cc5" />**2. Snap and seal the detector and get a dark enclosure**

<a id="3fed65dd82be80868213e7b69574fcf4" />The magnets allow the two halves to snap together securely, this means no more:

- <a id="3fed65dd82be80969b81f14cf3fd789d" />Figuring out complex fitting mechanisms.
- <a id="3fed65dd82be80298259d4d1273dbaad" />Spending time on lengthy assembly and disassembly.
- <a id="3fed65dd82be801684fdde993175dce1" />Struggling to keep each component securely in place.

<a id="3fed65dd82be8058a935db900dd202e7" />Slide in and out to detect fluorescence across all wells in one sitting!

<a id="3fed65dd82be80d7b287ff35797612c5" />Our holder is designed to fit the microfluidic chip snugly, minimizing unwanted movement and ensuring reliable fluorescence readings. The extruded legs on the bottom of the holder fit into the sliding tracks, allowing it to move smoothly through the detector.

<a id="3fed65dd82be807e9ff2c8522bfd5408" />How does the user know when a well reaches the detection site?

<a id="3fed65dd82be80839acdfd0f06dc12e2" />We incorporated two intuitive cues to guide the user through the detection process:

- <a id="3fed65dd82be800fa35de029d6f52cdd" />**Tactile feedback:** Small bumps on the sliding track create gentle vibrations as the holder moves past them, notifying users when a well reaches the detection site and when to pause for fluorescence measurement.
- <a id="3fed65dd82be8002b4e3e543679f33d7" />**Visual indicators:** White lines on the holder's handle mark the positions of individual wells. For example, when the second white line reaches the detector's wall, the second well is aligned with the fluorescence detection site.

<a id="3fed65dd82be804bb7c4fa0859d4fe48" />Together, these features make it easy to navigate between wells and complete fluorescence detection efficiently in a single session.

## <a id="3fbd65dd82be8055acb5c9d629e47f52" />Improved Detection Workflow

<a id="3fbd65dd82be80988d13d51f15f40090" />We studied the mechanism of fluorescence detection used in conventional detectors, and simplified them so they get the same task done at a way cheaper cost.

<a id="3fcd65dd82be80b1a2f2eb0a0ccfdead" />Most fluorescence detectors adopt a similar layout, with the illumination component, the sample, and the detection component forming a 90 degrees angle. This arrangement helps improve signal quality by reducing background noises <Cite refs={[{"n":3,"key":"DetectionMadeEasy2026"}]} /> . Excitation filters and emission filters permit a narrow band of wavelength, such that only the desired lights are filtered through and shone on the sample or detector <Cite refs={[{"n":7,"key":"ThorlabsFiltersFluorescence"}]} />.

<a id="3fcd65dd82be80bea79cf9c45374b2e6" />While this set-up has been widely approved, a critical limitation is the high cost of the filters. Since both filters need to allow a small range of light through, the precision determined their high price. After our research, we found that one excitation filter costs around \$300.

<a id="3fcd65dd82be80199d18ceb6e7dfc17b" />To address this issue, we identified the key purpose of each component, and reengineered the conventional detection workflow to a more succinct and cost-effective form. Leaving the most essential parts, our detector encompasses a single-color LED directly above the sample, and a longpass filter and photodetector directly below.

<a id="3fcd65dd82be803a80ffc5a7513e587a" />The function of each component is explained in detail in Design Breakdown. Overall, this layout reduced the material cost by at least 50% as the use of two filters was avoided. This customized set up saves a lot more space, and provides better accommodation for the microfluidics design <Cite refs={[{"n":8,"key":"DichroicLongpassFilters"}]} /> .

<details>
  <summary><a id="3fdd65dd82be80f6938fed3a667dc917" />Illumination</summary>

  <a id="3fdd65dd82be80638f6bf6e882df4e46" />We opted for a single color LED, which has a wavelength of 488nm. This acts as a simplified version of the light source + excitation filter combination. With lights emitted at a specific wavelength, the background noises are reduced and the signal quality is improved. The reason for choosing blue is because it is blue light has a shorter wavelength and higher frequency, which means it has higher energy. This makes it an effective choice for exciting fluorescence.
</details>

<details>
  <summary><a id="3fdd65dd82be80aab47ceea8c89c318d" />Filtering</summary>

  <a id="3fdd65dd82be805abe67fcdc440f1f0b" />A long pass filter is used as our emission filter. As its name indicates, a long pass filter blocks off lights of shorter wavelengths and lets lights of longer wavelengths pass through. We implemented a 500nm long pass filter to block off the blue light and permit green fluorescence, which is 510nm.
</details>

<details>
  <summary><a id="3fdd65dd82be804e8c33cf20247b7798" />Photodiode</summary>

  <a id="3fdd65dd82be8055b2b9e2b43f7070ed" />A photodiode converts received lights (photons) into electrical current. The signal will then be outputted to indicate if there is fluorescence or no.

<Callout icon="⚙️">
