# <a id="3ecd65dd82be8033802ad61ebd2244fe" />Introduction and Overview

<a id="3ecd65dd82be800680c3ffb1d7790e21" />Using a novel protocol developed by our hardware team, we fabricated a PDMS-based microfluidic chip from FDM-printed molds. This approach enabled us to produce customizable, low-cost chips that could be quickly iterated upon, combining the rapid prototyping of a 3D printer with the capabilities of PDMS-based microfluidics.

## <a id="3ecd65dd82be806182b5e89574979a70" />What is a microfluidic chip?

<a id="3ecd65dd82be8021b470f88b3f0261b1" />Microfluidic chips are an emerging technology within the biomedical space \[@pattanayakMicrofluidicChipsRecent2021] . They function by using a series of grooves less than a millimetre in diameter engraved into a wide variety of materials, including but not limited to PDMS and acrylic \[@pattanayakMicrofluidicChipsRecent2021], \[@whitesidesOriginsFutureMicrofluidics2006]  \[@sackmannPresentFutureRole2014].

<a id="3f8d65dd82be80329f21d5d40ae98bf1" />![](https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6279/wiki/assets/3f8d65dd82be80329f21d5d40ae98bf1.avif)

<a id="3f8d65dd82be80e9acc5d959143e5f41" />Image from:\[@liuSimpleDirectReading2015]

<a id="3f9d65dd82be80cd85a7c6b8012e7fe8" />Microfluidic chips are especially valuable in biomedical engineering due to the scale of the chips, leading to laminar flow \[@whitesidesOriginsFutureMicrofluidics2006]. Laminar flow is valuable to engineers

## <a id="3ecd65dd82be80da9a8fcc670521c91a" />How does a microfluidic chip integrate into our project?

<a id="3ecd65dd82be805bbca8ec1ec15b7aca" />The microfluidic chip is the epicentre of the project, where everything comes together. Early on in the conception of the project, we realized that we needed a platform that could serve the purposes of:

- <a id="3f8d65dd82be80ccbc93d767ceb9b364" />Mixing the antibiotic and patient sample
- <a id="3f8d65dd82be80529b43caa304dde353" />Hold the sample and antibiotic until the degradation time has elapsed
- <a id="3f8d65dd82be80e1bc61ce995bb4f216" />Move the mixed antibiotic and patient sample to our sensor bacteria
- <a id="3f8d65dd82be802da9cfcca63d63161a" />Quantify whether or not fluorescence is present

# <a id="3ecd65dd82be80a98a75ed92ac7b1687" />Design Considerations

<a id="3ecd65dd82be8039b3bedf46a9028251" />When we were brainstorming for this project, this was the list we generated:

- <a id="3f8d65dd82be80c3a420f0ac793c4ef2" />low cost
- <a id="3f8d65dd82be805f9c20fa4da2b317eb" />no leakage
- <a id="3f8d65dd82be8015aa00c7b0bfdd12f5" />minimizes (preferably none) air bubbles
- <a id="3f8d65dd82be80efb82dcca39c536cf0" />needs to be able to fill with the desired fluid easily
  - <a id="3f8d65dd82be802389bfe34b7b5dd69e" />ie connectable to tubing
- <a id="3f8d65dd82be80fc8c3bed5d831f7b5b" />needs to connect with an external monitor or have some way to output user data that is needed
- <a id="3f8d65dd82be8037beebf9a103af209b" />Channels must allow fluids to behave the way they naturally do without restriction
  - <a id="3f8d65dd82be80bda614eb69c382f1b1" />ie. channel diameter should not restrict mixing or seperation
- <a id="3f8d65dd82be8046a5ebf7b5f1f1e12c" />manufacturing material must not be reactive with the fluid or be absorbent
  - <a id="3f8d65dd82be8056abd4d757e9fa437d" />chemically and biologically compatible
- <a id="3f8d65dd82be80a2bc6ade7f4e80aef2" />preferably transparent material for observation

# <a id="3f9d65dd82be8063ab9fe12333edc041" />Design Decisions

<a id="3f9d65dd82be80658962f652cb40997e" />Before designing our microfluidic chip, we needed to select a suitable manufacturing process that balanced cost, optical transparency, and leak resistance. Since multiple prototypes would be required throughout development, low fabrication costs were essential to support iterative design improvements. The chip also needed to be transparent for fluorescence imaging and reliably sealed to prevent fluid leakage and ensure consistent operation. Based on our research into common microfluidic fabrication techniques, we compiled the comparison table below to evaluate each method and identify the most suitable approach for our application.

<a id="3f9d65dd82be80f0a5ccd0d5129b9c4d" />

| <a id="3f9d65dd82be80c98f02e8586ee970c8" />**Requirement**                        | **FDM + Epoxy + PDMS**                                                        | **PMMA (CNC / Parylene-coated)**                     | **Xurography**                                                     |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be80c3bdccfe0450e76d98" />**Cost per chip**                      | \$1–5                                                                         | \$10–100                                             | Very low (cheap prototyping)                                       |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be80ba85c8cd79d9d16737" />**Leakage resistance**                 | High if properly bonded & plasma treated                                      | High if well machined & sealed                       | Moderate; depends on adhesive and lamination quality               |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be80fea87cf70cb72941f7" />**Air bubble formation**               | Low if degassed properly                                                      | Low; less of an issue with rigid material            | Moderate to high; manual filling can trap bubbles                  |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be8076b38bc3ed4c4ba491" />**Ease of filling / tubing interface** | Can integrate standard tubing; inlet/outlet sizing must be carefully designed | Easy; CNC allows precise inlet/outlet                | Easy for simple channels; limited precision for connections        |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be80638918fb5a4d3dd123" />**Volume capacity**                    | Small to medium; depends on mold design                                       | Small to medium; CNC allows variable volumes         | Small; best for micro-scale flows                                  |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be803b8942c721790731a8" />**Data interfacing**                   | Compatible with optical or sensor integration; transparent                    | Can be compatible; transparent                       | Limited; usually only optical observation possible                 |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be80689a99ed4f2fdf7eb9" />**Fluid behavior / channel fidelity**  | High fidelity if mold is precise                                              | High; CNC allows precise dimensions                  | Moderate; cutting precision may limit channel shape and smoothness |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be8028bef4d7c9b40b20d9" />**Material compatibility**             | PDMS: chemically inert, biocompatible                                         | PMMA: chemically resistant if coated; biocompatible  | Depends on adhesive and film; may absorb some chemicals            |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be80189ff5f2222c56ba7a" />**Transparency / observation**         | Excellent                                                                     | Excellent                                            | Good to moderate, depends on film used                             |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be8008976dd67edeb41a83" />**Feasibility in lab / prototyping**   | Medium; requires vacuum, plasma, clean workspace                              | Medium; CNC access required, coating adds complexity | Very high; fast and cheap, good for early prototypes               |

<a id="3f9d65dd82be8011ba67ffd711426eba" />\[@5TechniquesMake2021] \[@liuMicrofluidicsChipsFabrication2024a]

<a id="3f9d65dd82be8098b4f4dc7229f5b55e" />As xurography could not be done transparently, we removed it as a contender for the project. We then made a weighted decision matrix to allow us to make a more objective decision between PMMA and PDMS.

<a id="3f9d65dd82be807d8368ec1fea89bf89" />

|                                                                 |            |          |              |          |              |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- | -------- | ------------ | -------- | ------------ |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be80fc909ef23ae1bd3103" />**Criteria**         | **Weight** | **PDMS** | **weighted** | **PMMA** | **weighted** |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be80a8b679eca07b135a9b" />Cost/Chip            | 30         | 10       | 3            | 3        | 0.9          |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be80f8b381d6d4697f613e" />Volume Capacity      | 5          | 9        | 0.45         | 9        | 0.45         |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be8021ae4df1192bffcede" />Time Intensity       | 10         | 8        | 0.8          | 8        | 0.8          |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be80e69cdbd2bc1379f717" />Creation Complexity  | 15         | 5        | 0.75         | 5        | 0.75         |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be807da88bfb3c9ae515e9" />Air bubble formation | 5          | 5        | 0.25         | 10       | 0.5          |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be80e58714d4e039241259" />Leakage              | 15         | 6        | 0.9          | 7        | 1.05         |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be80ffa030e11f26338732" />Equipment Required   | 20         | 3        | 0.6          | 9        | 1.8          |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be8016a01ad687ed0f1cd7" />**TOTAL**            | 100        |          | 6.75         |          | 6.25         |

<a id="3f9d65dd82be805b852ad2e6618d20aa" />Due to the WDM, we were able to see that, in our circumstances, PDMS was a superior choice to PMMA. It should be noted that PDMS would have been a more expensive choice for us, considering the limited number of prototypes we were making compared to the price of Sylgard PDMS, had it not been kindly donated to the team by one of our advisors.
