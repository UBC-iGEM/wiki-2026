# <a id="3ecd65dd82be8033802ad61ebd2244fe" />Introduction

<a id="3ecd65dd82be800680c3ffb1d7790e21" />Using a novel protocol developed by our hardware team, we fabricated a polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS)-based microfluidic chip from fused deposition modelling (FDM)-printed molds. This approach enabled us to produce customizable, low-cost chips that could be quickly iterated upon, combining the rapid prototyping of a 3D printer with the capabilities of PDMS-based microfluidics.

## <a id="3ecd65dd82be806182b5e89574979a70" />What is a microfluidic chip?

<a id="3ecd65dd82be8021b470f88b3f0261b1" />Microfluidic chips are an emerging technology within the biomedical space <Cite refs={[{"n":1,"key":"pattanayakMicrofluidicChipsRecent2021"}]} /> . They function by using a series of grooves less than a millimetre in diameter engraved into a wide variety of materials, including but not limited to PDMS and acrylic <Cite refs={[{"n":1,"key":"pattanayakMicrofluidicChipsRecent2021"},{"n":2,"key":"whitesidesOriginsFutureMicrofluidics2006"},{"n":3,"key":"sackmannPresentFutureRole2014"}]} />.

## <a id="3ecd65dd82be80da9a8fcc670521c91a" />How does a microfluidic chip integrate into our project?

<a id="3ecd65dd82be805bbca8ec1ec15b7aca" />The microfluidic chip is the epicentre of the project, where everything comes together. Early on in the conception of the project, we realized that we needed a platform that could serve the purposes of:

- <a id="3f8d65dd82be80ccbc93d767ceb9b364" />Mixing the antibiotic and patient sample
- <a id="3f8d65dd82be80529b43caa304dde353" />Hold the sample and antibiotic until the degradation time has elapsed
- <a id="3f8d65dd82be80e1bc61ce995bb4f216" />Move the mixed antibiotic and patient sample to our sensor bacteria
- <a id="3f8d65dd82be802da9cfcca63d63161a" />Quantify whether or not fluorescence is present

<a id="3f9d65dd82be80d69e3aced35483f3fb" />We felt that a microfluidic chip would allow us to approach the above in the fewest steps possible, so a clinician using the device would not need to monitor or work with it as closely, enabling them to run multiple experiments in parallel for different patients.

## <a id="3fdd65dd82be8042b2dfdef3fd87c4ab" />Design Considerations

<a id="3ecd65dd82be8039b3bedf46a9028251" />Our team decided to start the process by using a development framework developed by \[enter]. This helped ensure we made thoughtful decisions as we approached our microfluidic chip. We started the project by brainstorming the requirements our chip would have to meet to be considered a functional product.  They can be seen in the list below:

- <a id="3f8d65dd82be80c3a420f0ac793c4ef2" />Must be low cost
- <a id="3f8d65dd82be805f9c20fa4da2b317eb" />Must have zero leakage
- <a id="3f8d65dd82be8015aa00c7b0bfdd12f5" />Must minimize air bubbles in the manufactured material and input fluid
- <a id="3f8d65dd82be80efb82dcca39c536cf0" />Must be able to be filled with a 1 mL of fluid
- <a id="3f8d65dd82be80fc8c3bed5d831f7b5b" />Must be able to integrate a device that will output relevant data
- <a id="3f8d65dd82be8046a5ebf7b5f1f1e12c" />Must be manufactured from a material that is inert and biologically compatible
- <a id="3f8d65dd82be80a2bc6ade7f4e80aef2" />Must be transparent to allow for viewing of fluorescence

<a id="3f9d65dd82be806babfacbf3c7c7fe61" />With this list in mind, we were able to approach the selection of a manufacturing process.

## <a id="3f9d65dd82be8063ab9fe12333edc041" />Manufacturing Decisions

<a id="3f9d65dd82be80658962f652cb40997e" />Before designing the channels of our microfluidic chip, we needed to select a suitable manufacturing process that balanced cost, optical transparency, and leak resistance.  We heavily prioritized cost, as a low fabrication cost was essential to support iterative design improvements. The chip also needed to be transparent for fluorescence imaging and reliably sealed to prevent fluid leakage. Based on our research of common microfluidic fabrication techniques, we compiled the comparison table below to evaluate each method and identify the most suitable approach for our application.

<a id="3f9d65dd82be80f0a5ccd0d5129b9c4d" />

| <a id="3f9d65dd82be80c98f02e8586ee970c8" />**Requirement**                        | **FDM + Epoxy + PDMS**                                                        | **PMMA (CNC / Parylene-coated)**                     | **Xurography**                                                     |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be80c3bdccfe0450e76d98" />**Cost per chip**                      | \$1–5                                                                         | \$10–100                                             | Very low (cheap prototyping)                                       |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be80ba85c8cd79d9d16737" />**Leakage resistance**                 | High if properly bonded & plasma treated                                      | High if well machined & sealed                       | Moderate; depends on adhesive and lamination quality               |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be80fea87cf70cb72941f7" />**Air bubble formation**               | Low if degassed properly                                                      | Low; less of an issue with rigid material            | Moderate to high; manual filling can trap bubbles                  |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be8076b38bc3ed4c4ba491" />**Ease of filling / tubing interface** | Can integrate standard tubing; inlet/outlet sizing must be carefully designed | Easy; CNC allows precise inlet/outlet                | Easy for simple channels; limited precision for connections        |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be80638918fb5a4d3dd123" />**Volume capacity**                    | Small to medium; depends on mold design                                       | Small to medium; CNC allows variable volumes         | Small; best for micro-scale flows                                  |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be803b8942c721790731a8" />**Data interfacing**                   | Compatible with optical or sensor integration; transparent                    | Can be compatible; transparent                       | Limited; usually only optical observation possible                 |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be80689a99ed4f2fdf7eb9" />**Fluid behavior / channel fidelity**  | High fidelity if mold is precise                                              | High; CNC allows precise dimensions                  | Moderate; cutting precision may limit channel shape and smoothness |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be8028bef4d7c9b40b20d9" />**Material compatibility**             | PDMS: chemically inert, biocompatible                                         | PMMA: chemically resistant if coated; biocompatible  | Depends on adhesive and film; may absorb some chemicals            |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be80189ff5f2222c56ba7a" />**Transparency / observation**         | Excellent                                                                     | Excellent                                            | Good to moderate, depends on film used                             |
| <a id="3f9d65dd82be8008976dd67edeb41a83" />**Feasibility in lab / prototyping**   | Medium; requires a desiccator and plasma treater                              | Medium; CNC access required, coating adds complexity | Very high; fast and cheap, good for early prototypes               |

    <a id="3fcd65dd82be80a3ad97c680c34c73cf" />Three separate sections were designed to be put into the epoxy chip, corresponding to a fluid mixing section, a degradation section, and an outlet section.     <a id="3fcd65dd82be802088ebd4abe4b7498e" />For this first iteration of chips, a zigzag pattern was chosen to represent the mixing section of the epoxy chip. ## <a id="3f9d65dd82be80248af7fe362fb89f9b" />Mark Six: Linear Channels

<a id="3f9d65dd82be80d09733e1fd08769961" />

<Dbtl>
  <Fragment slot="design">
    <a id="3f9d65dd82be809bb910fd33cfb5f9cd" />We changed the position of our channels to make integration with the fluorescence detector significantly easier as the angle between the wells on the half circle approach led to a very small angular difference in the wells and would require complex and highly accurate mechanics to differentiate between different wells.
  </Fragment>

  <Fragment slot="build">
    <a id="3fdd65dd82be805db712e3a689d208d4" />

    <Figure imgs={[{"url":"https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6279/wiki/assets/1b29d447260c5009836ad07894bba3b8.avif","alt":"","scale":0}]}>
      <Fragment slot="content">
        <a id="3fdd65dd82be80c3b26cdb1058ad01bc" />The dark circles on this image represent the wells, and the light grey rectangle represents the microfluidic chip. Our dimensions are visualized using the brackets.
      </Fragment>
    </Figure>
  </Fragment>

  <Fragment slot="test">
    <a id="3f9d65dd82be80ed869af70d70f2625d" />To determine that our linear wells would not lead to a significant difference in filling (so that the bacteria are not exposed to different levels of antibiotic) we performed a series of calculations to see what the difference would be.

    <a id="3fcd65dd82be8039a978d3d2041a6d15" />Assumptions:

    - <a id="3fcd65dd82be805a8dbfd8e8767fe941" />main channel is 1\*1 mm\*2
    - <a id="3fcd65dd82be8096822ec1c247ed5f22" />outlets (7) are 0.5\*0.5 mm^2
    - <a id="3fcd65dd82be8034a073d6607e9ab062" />outlet length is 15 mm
    - <a id="3fcd65dd82be80a7a9c5ec76a30d78c6" />outlet spacing 8 mm apart
    - <a id="3fcd65dd82be8025bc32fe9c9bcc6e2f" />identical outlets
    - <a id="3fcd65dd82be805fbfc6d3849eb7f023" />There is no output from the wells (i.e. they never overflow)

    <br id="3fcd65dd82be80809613c10e4d7a1359" />

    <a id="3fcd65dd82be8078a0befc14c9192da1" />Hydraulic Resistance of main channel:

    <a id="3fcd65dd82be804382d6f21b8f19f2bd" />

    $$
    \mu: \text{dynamic viscosity},
    L: \text{Length} \\ 
    R_{M} = \frac{28.4 \mu \cdot L}{h^{4}} \\
    R_{M} = \frac{28.4 (0.001) \cdot(0.008)}{(0.001)^{4}} \\
    R_{M} = 2.3 \cdot 10^8 \frac{Pa\cdot s}{m^{3}}

    $$

    <a id="3fcd65dd82be8066956df04399fbfbfe" />Hydraulic Resistance of the Outlet Channel

    <a id="3fcd65dd82be80168360e1a4d281f96e" />

    $$
    R_{O} = \frac{28.4 \mu*L}{h^{4}} \\
    R_{O} = \frac{28.4 (0.001)\cdot(0.0015)}{(0.0005)^{4}} \\
    R_{O} = 6.8 \cdot 10^{9} \frac{Pa \cdot s}{m^{3}}

    $$

    <a id="3fcd65dd82be80749853e50f1960f236" />The outlet channel is therefore 300x more resistive than the main channel.

    <a id="3fcd65dd82be803da847db7267f7b901" />

    $$
    \frac{R_{O}}{R_{M}} = 30
    $$

    <a id="3fcd65dd82be80f5804fe717aa249165" />To split our flow equally between wells we must divide the flow between the eight wells.

    <a id="3fcd65dd82be80ca97e1d6f6cd32db2d" />

    $$
    Q_{i}=\frac{Q_{O}}{7} = 0.143 Q_{O}
    $$

    <a id="3fcd65dd82be80a098ccd61c547c2ae9" />In the main channel, the flow will drop as it passes each successive well.

    <a id="3fcd65dd82be801184acd1a733986ae7" />

    $$
    Q= 1 \text{,} \frac{6}{7} \text{,} \frac{5}{7} \text{,} \frac{4}{7}\text{,} \frac{3}{7}\text{,} \frac{2}{7}\text{,}\frac{1}{7}
    $$

    <a id="3fcd65dd82be8041a2c7dfd8af79e1cf" />In the main channel, there were therefore be a pressure loss as the volume of fluid pushing forward will be lessened:

    <a id="3fcd65dd82be806faf91d61bbfce89d9" />

    $$
    \Delta P = QR_{M} \\
    \begin{array}{|c|c|c|}
    \hline
    \text{Well} & \text{Flow Rate} & \text{Pressure Drop} \\
    \hline
    1 & Q & QR_m \\
    2 & \frac{6}{7}Q & \frac{6}{7}QR_m \\
    3 & \frac{5}{7}Q & \frac{5}{7}QR_m \\
    4 & \frac{4}{7}Q & \frac{4}{7}QR_m \\
    5 & \frac{3}{7}Q & \frac{3}{7}QR_m \\
    6 & \frac{2}{7}Q & \frac{2}{7}QR_m \\
    7 & \frac{1}{7}Q & \frac{1}{7}QR_m \\
    \hline
    \end{array}
    $$

    <a id="3fcd65dd82be80b1a713e061cc2ce7b7" />You are then able to add up all of the pressure drops.

    <a id="3fcd65dd82be80acbb5cefe1ca9bf0b1" />

    $$
    \sum_{i=P_{1}}^{P_{8}} \Delta P_{Mi} = Q_{i}R_{M} \\
    \sum_{i=P_{1}}^{P_{8}} \Delta P_{Mi} = (1+\frac{6}{7}+\frac{5}{7}+\frac{4}{7}+\frac{3}{7}+\frac{2}{7}+\frac{1}{7})QR_{M} \\
    \Delta P_M =  9.2\cdot 10^8 Q Pa
    $$

    <a id="3fcd65dd82be80858440d9eae070c516" />To find the pressure drops across the outlets as fluid moves in we then:

    <a id="3fcd65dd82be8058a844d5f4fdc4bbed" />

    $$
    R_{O}=6.8 \cdot 10^{9} \\
    \Delta P_O = (7)(R_{O})(\frac{Q} {7})\\
    \Delta P_O = 6.8 \cdot 10^{9} Q Pa
    $$

    <a id="3fcd65dd82be80129549e5c56d71678f" />We can then compare the pressure difference across the main channel to into the outlets to find whether there is an unequal push.

    <a id="3fcd65dd82be8070addff8c6b0f05683" />

    $$
    \frac{\Delta P_M}{\Delta P_O}=\frac{9.2\cdot 10^8}{6.8 \cdot10^{9}} \\
    \frac{\Delta P_M}{\Delta P_O}= 0.14\\
    $$

    <a id="3fdd65dd82be80638303cf04ac758c5f" />Therefore, most of the pressure comes from the outlet. Because the pressure at the outlet is so much larger than the pressure in the main channel, the flow tends to equalize.4\\

    <a id="3fdd65dd82be8081ae26cb5abf2fac45" />We can now compare individual wells to see if there is a difference in that pressure equalization. If there is a significant difference it implies that there is a significant difference in the filling of the wells.

    <a id="3fdd65dd82be80afb6abdcb439ecee95" />

    $$
    \frac{ \Delta P_{M}}{\Delta P_{O7}}=\frac{(Q/7)R_M}{R_OQ} \\
    \frac{\Delta P_{M}}{\Delta P_{O7}}=\frac{R_M}{7R_O} \\
    \frac{\Delta P_{M}}{\Delta P_{O7}}=0.00476

    $$

    <a id="3fdd65dd82be808182aed902a1bf1d5a" />We then do this for each outlet:

    <a id="3fdd65dd82be800e97ebf7d9a43c5a13" />

    $$
    \begin{array}{|c|c|}
    \hline
    \text{Section} &
    \text{Pressure Difference With Main Channel} \\
    \hline
    1 & 0.0333\\
    2 &  0.0167\\
    3 &  0.0111\\
    4 &  0.0083 \\
    5 &  0.0067\\
    6 &  0.0056\\
    7 &  0.0048\\
    8 &   0.0042\\
    \hline
    \end{array}
    $$

    <a id="3fdd65dd82be804da412cad0f9c2b3bf" />We can now determine the relative variation between the first outlet and the last outlet:

    <a id="3fdd65dd82be8049ba6af6653ee9eb7d" />

    $$
    \frac{P_{O7}}{P_{O1}}=\frac{0.0042}{0.0333} = 0.13
    $$

    <a id="3fdd65dd82be80a3bce7cef3dbcdc4cb" />Therefore, the relative difference between the first well and the last well is 13% since pressure and flow have a direct relationship.
  </Fragment>

  <Fragment slot="learn">
    <a id="3f9d65dd82be80eaa0b3f6a57715b681" />For our final iteration of the device, we wanted to reduce the number of channels to remove redundancy during flow testing.
  </Fragment>

# <a id="3ecd65dd82be803eb079c678c4df61d6" />Next Steps

<a id="3fcd65dd82be80f8bcd9f324e37d227c" />Our next steps with this project will be optimizing our input flow rates and updating our process so that we are able to get more than one chip out of the same mold. Optimizing the input flow rates of the sample and antibiotic will allow us to maximize the response from our biosensors. Currently, we have been breaking our molds in order to get our chips out; however, for both environmental and monetary reasons, it would be more optimal if we were able to get multiple chips out of the same mold.
