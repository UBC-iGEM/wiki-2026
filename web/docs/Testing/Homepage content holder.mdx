<a id="3ffd65dd82be80a6be23d6f4f729708f" />When every second counts, guessing isn’t enough. FAST sets a higher standard, bringing antibiotic susceptibility testing to the point of care, so the right drug reaches the patient sooner.

# <a id="3fdd65dd82be80d9b3afc6b7d4fb6d33" />The Rebel Bacteria

<a id="3fdd65dd82be80f19512c914ce8d6b0b" />Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) is one of the fastest-growing threats to global health. Today, standard antibiotic susceptibility testing takes days and usually relies on non-clinical services, so clinicians are often prescribing broad-spectrum antibiotics before they know what will work. Every unnecessary and ineffective prescription costs patients time and fuels resistance.

# <a id="3fcd65dd82be80afb02ecf242a9db481" />What is antibiotic susceptibility?

<a id="3fdd65dd82be8022a20de8c7acf1478f" />**Antibiotic susceptibility is how easily a given antibiotic can either stop a bacterium from growing or kill it.** It is measured by using **antibiotic susceptibility testing (AST)**, which often finds the lowest drug concentration that stops visible bacterial growth. The result places the bacteria into one of three categories: **susceptible, intermediate, or resistant**. This tells clinicians whether the drug is likely to work at a dose the patient can safely receive.

<a id="3fdd65dd82be802491bde9b890c66795" />**Susceptibility can change over time and antibiotic use favors bacteria that can survive it.** Bacteria can gain resistance either through genetic mutations or pick up resistance genes from other bacteria, often on small mobile pieces of DNA called **plasmids**. These resistant strains survive antibiotic treatments and can then reproduce and spread, making these bacteria difficult to control. Every unnecessary or poorly targeted prescription therefore speeds up the process of resistance evolution, decreasing the number of effective drugs for the  bacteria. Resistance differs between patients, pathogens, and regions, so the only reliable way to know whether an antibiotic will work for a specific infection is to test it. Faster, more widely available AST, with results ready before the prescription is written rather than days later, is key to both treating patients effectively and to slowing down antimicrobial resistance.

# <a id="3fcd65dd82be804493f1d215d81bcf9b" />\[under microfluidic chip]

<a id="3fcd65dd82be80a0acfcf85c9d1373e7" />Using microfluidic technology, we can reduce the amount of patient sample needed or perform more tests using the same amount of sample. Combined with user-friendliness and accessibility at point-of-care, we can enable non-specialists to conduct quicker testing for a variety of different antibiotics.\
\
There are many different antibiotics, modes of resistance, and resistant strains of bacteria. This year, we’re tackling **urinary tract infections** (UTIs), specifically *E. coli* that degrade antibiotics like β-lactams and aminoglycosides. We’ll be looking at two antibiotics from each class and developing a prototype capable of testing for resistance and reporting it using fluorescence.
