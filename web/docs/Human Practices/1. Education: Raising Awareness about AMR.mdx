Education became an important extension of our work. As we explored the social dimensions of AMR through our literature review and iHPs, we began thinking more broadly about how education can contribute to greater awareness and understanding of the issue.
Our literature review helped us identify adolescents as an important audience for AMR education. Research showed that misinformation surrounding antimicrobial mechanisms was concentrated among young adults of ages 18-24 as well as those with a high school degree or less (Crago et al, 2022). These findings shaped an important direction for this branch of our work: building an understanding of AMR early in life. To explore this, we partnered with UBC Geering Up Engineering Outreach during their K-12 summer camps to introduce AMR through accessible and engaging environment.
However, we wanted our workshops to be more than just a presentation of information. We took an active learning approach, encouraging question asking and incorporating interactive activities.
