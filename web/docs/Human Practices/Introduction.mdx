Rachael:<br/><br/>Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) is often discussed through a clinical lens: How pathogens cause it, what treatments are effective, and how the numbers are rising. However, behind every statistic and every resistant infection, there is a person with real emotions, struggles, and a desire to be understood. Their experiences <span discussion-urls="discussion\://3d7d65dd-82be-80cb-bb5c-d6fb4854c487/3dcd65dd-82be-8063-b3d3-dc894ec7a68d/3dfd65dd-82be-80ba-855d-001c2445149f">can extend far beyond the biology.</span>
Our project aimed to broaden the impact of AMR research by addressing the discrepancies experienced by AMR patients in our society as well as identifying knowledge gaps within the general public. Despite the common notion about AMR pertaining to physiological burdens, past research has shown that many of these patients struggle with downstream socioeconomic inequities and ability to acquire sufficient care. Moreover, further psychological and physical limitations arise as a result of AMR, ranging from the potential pathogenic nature of patients’ infection preventing them from seeing their loved ones for extended periods of time or the patient becoming immobile and thus, bed-ridden. To implement more effective policies locally to address some of these challenges, it is first important to understand what patient populations are affected, in what ways they are affected and ultimately give them a voice to speak their truth.
Thus, we conducted a literature review to investigate what is currently known about AMR, examining the patient populations represented in existing research (especially in BC) and the <span discussion-urls="discussion\://3d7d65dd-82be-80cb-bb5c-d6fb4854c487/3e5d65dd-82be-80f2-8f3e-d33abb669d58/3dfd65dd-82be-80e8-9b11-001c02556fa4">barriers</span> they are described to face. In doing so, we aimed not only to understand what evidence currently exists, but also to identify where important perspectives may still be missing. This was supplemented with anecdotal evidence from infectious disease physicians, nurses, and researchers gained from interviews. Ultimately, our aim was to disseminate our findings regarding the medical, socioeconomic, sex-oriented, and psychosocial barriers of these individuals with the greater Canadian diaspora via partnering with institutions such as Fraser Health, working with UBC Geering Up to share our findings with younger populations, and implementing a digital media campaign by way of a website and Instagram to further echo the voices of patients and physicians.
On the whole, this initiative aims to unmask the hidden struggles experienced by patients with antimicrobial resistance as an effort to raise awareness for implementing effective measures that alleviate their struggles, the culminated evidence of which is shared with an official <span discussion-urls="discussion\://3d7d65dd-82be-80cb-bb5c-d6fb4854c487/3d7d65dd-82be-80a8-8497-f9196b33a6e0/3e5d65dd-82be-804b-89d5-001cce43c00a">government-affiliated institution </span>that can make the decision to use our findings to serve a greater good.
