<a id="3dcd65dd82be80519a4be150c21a0020" />Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) is often discussed through a clinical lens: how pathogens cause it, what treatments are effective, and how the numbers are rising. However, behind every statistic and every resistant infection, there is a person with real emotions, struggles, and a desire to be understood. Their experiences can extend far beyond the biology.

<a id="3f8d65dd82be8099a08fe09af8389e01" />A curiosity sparked for our team early on during the 2026 iGEM season: we read a preliminary article about the psychosocial impacts of AMR on patients. However, little did we know this would soon became a purpose to understand the person behind an AMR diagnosis. If patients are feeling distressed, anxious and afraid, why are they feeling these emotions and how could we help? To better understand this, we conducted a thorough literature review to investigate what is currently known about AMR, examining the patient populations represented in existing research and the barriers they face. In doing so, we aimed not only to understand what evidence currently exists, but also to identify where important perspectives may still be missing.

<a id="3f8d65dd82be80828f0fde6cef50323b" />To our surprise, there was no literature in British Columbia (BC) concerning the unique experiences of AMR patients. So to understand what barriers exist for these patients, we reached out to with infectious disease physicians, nurses, and researchers. This is where we realized that under the lens of our team wanting to ameliorate compassionate care, there was a web of interconnected barriers arising from a lack of adequate informational support for patients and families about the scientific underpinnings of AMR. From our outreach to patients and experts, we learned that patients were, for the most part, left on their own in understanding their condition and the lack of information coming to them from their healthcare institutions was related to feelings of distress and isolation, the very emotions that led us to conducting our research. AMR in many cases had contributed to isolation, further preventing patients from interacting with those in the same boat to learn about the science of AMR.

<a id="3f8d65dd82be8096b0c8c6818f191c9d" />Thus, with patients, their families, and the general public being our audience, our aim became to address this informational barrier by disseminating our findings on the nature of AMR. We also wanted to address misconceptions about it with the greater Canadian diaspora through Fraser Health and UBC Geering Up. We worked with our patient partners and experts to do the aforementioned while implementing a digital media campaign by way of a website and Instagram to further echo the voices of patients, bringing accurate information about AMR out of the dark. To address the secondary barrier of isolation which was related to the primary barrier of inadequate exchange of knowledge about the scientific basis of AMR amongst patients with similar lived experiences, we designed an interactive platform on our website so that patients can communicate with those in the same boat as them.

<a id="3d7d65dd82be80a88497f9196b33a6e0" />On the whole, these initiatives aim to provide accurate, expert information about the scientific basis of AMR to patients, their families and the general public to alleviate the psychosocial stressors patients face. We also hope to continue our work beyond the iGEM jamboree to ensure that our resources reach as many patients and family members as possible.
