To broaden the impact of our project by addressing the discrepancies experienced by AMR patients in the society.
The Antimicrobial Resistance Project (ARP) serves to take a systematic approach to understanding the struggles of underrepresented patient populations with antimicrobial resistance (AMR). Despite the common notion about AMR pertaining to physiological burdens, past research has shown that many of these patients struggle with downstream socioeconomic inequities and ability to acquire sufficient care. Moreover, further psychological and physical limitations arise as a result of AMR, ranging from the potential pathogenic nature of patients’ infection preventing them from seeing their loved ones for extended periods of time or the patient becoming immobile and thus, bed-ridden. To implement more effective policies locally to address some of these challenges, it is first important to understand what patient populations are affected, in what ways they are affected and ultimately give them a voice to speak their truth. Thus, we conducted a thorough literature search on patient populations with antimicrobial resistance within BC and complemented our findings with anecdotal evidence from infectious disease physicians, nurses, ~~social workers~~ and researchers. <span discussion-urls="discussion\://3d7d65dd-82be-80cb-bb5c-d6fb4854c487/3d7d65dd-82be-8056-af04-d49854ee54f5/3dfd65dd-82be-80ad-8516-001cb5a2fac9">More importantly, our aim was to disseminate our findings regarding the medical, socioeconomic, sex-oriented, and psychosocial barriers of these individuals with the greater Canadian diaspora via partnering with government institutions such as the BCCDC, working with UBC Geering Up to share our findings with younger populations and implementing a social media campaign to further echo the voices of patients and physicians. </span>
On the whole, this initiative aims to unmask the hidden struggles experienced by patients with antimicrobial resistance as an effort to raise awareness for implementing effective measures that alleviate their struggles, the culminated evidence of which is shared with an official government-affiliated institution that can make the decision to use our findings to serve a greater good.
