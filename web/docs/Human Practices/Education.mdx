# <a id="3fdd65dd82be807d8711db7d8f2703c8" />Overview

<a id="3fdd65dd82be8069a944f67980960404" />UBC iGEM aims to spread the exciting applications and techniques of general synthetic biology to a diverse audience. Beyond building an understanding for AMR in relation to our project, the Human Practices team designed synthetic biology educational resources and workshops for primary to secondary school students, marginalized adults, and within our own UBC iGEM team.

<a id="3fdd65dd82be8072955ac0f19331f202" />To start our season, we recognized that new and returning members of our multidisciplinary team would benefit from learning the essential laboratory techniques and safety that our wet lab team carries out every year. To streamline the training process, the human practices and wet lab team collaborated to create a video series (\~1-5 minutes per video) on basic techniques, such that members can familiarize with them asynchronously and look back on for review. ## <a id="3fdd65dd82be807b971ee54571a28c9f" />Youreka Vancouver Symposium

<a id="3fdd65dd82be804c9933fecfa6766d21" />Youreka is a program that pairs high school students with undergraduate mentors across universities in Canada to create research projects and present them at regional symposiums. The human practices team gave a keynote speech at the Youreka Vancouver Symposium to introduce the high school participants to the UBC iGEM team, garner interest in synthetic biology, and connect our project to their research topic on drugs.

## <a id="3fdd65dd82be80bd8c4ec01fca4d5b72" />UBC Science 101

<a id="3fdd65dd82be8019a426d01d8f4ee7ba" />Expanding our audience to the adult population, Science 101 is a free course offered by UBC’s Faculty of Science to residents of Vancouver’s Downtown Eastside and surrounding communities who are interested in learning about science. We collaborated with the teaching team to deliver a hands-on tutorial to synthetic biology and BioBrick design. The workshop, “Building BioBricks”, introduces students to basic concepts of genetic engineering, plasmid design, and the iGEM BioBrick, building on their previous lecture topics on DNA. Students then grouped into teams, where they used lego bricks to model parts (promoter, ribosome binding site, gene of interest, and terminator) and build their own fluorescent biosensor to detect microplastics in drinking water. The concept of the iGEM Parts Registry was also modelled to show students how they can swap out and replace parts with other teams to use in their own gene circuits.

<a id="3fdd65dd82be80be9b10c895b2a654cc" />During the session, the adult students were very engaged, constantly asking questions and expressing curiosity. During the activity, students worked collaboratively to troubleshoot their faulty sensors and come up with a solution. We believe the activity and topic of BioBricks was introduced at an appropriate difficulty level, as many of the students expressed interest in synthetic biology and our project afterwards.

## <a id="3fdd65dd82be80a88b3cce0c2b23e4f3" />seed2STEM

<a id="3fdd65dd82be8002aeb0e8fe414e7b72" />seed2STEM is a summer research program for Indigenous high school students in Metro Vancouver and Kelowna, hosted by UBC International Collaboration on Repair Discoveries (iCORD). Students participated on a tour of UBC School of Biomedical Engineering (SBME)’s new facility, the Gordan B. Shrum Building on UBC campus, where UBC iGEM was invited to deliver a hardware demo and participate in a student panel. We introduced the Indigenous students to what our team does, our project this year, and our dry lab team demonstrated our in-house bioreactors and microfluidic chips. Our team members then participated in a panel, where students asked questions on university, biomedical engineering, and iGEM experience.
