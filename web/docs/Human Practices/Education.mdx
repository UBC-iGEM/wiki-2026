# <a id="3fdd65dd82be807d8711db7d8f2703c8" />Overview

<a id="3fdd65dd82be8069a944f67980960404" />UBC iGEM aims to spread the exciting applications and techniques of general synthetic biology to a diverse audience. Beyond building an understanding for AMR in relation to our project, the Human Practices team designed synthetic biology educational resources and workshops for primary to secondary school students, marginalized adults, and within our own UBC iGEM team.

<a id="3fdd65dd82be8072955ac0f19331f202" />To start our season, we recognized that new and returning members of our multidisciplinary team would benefit from learning the essential laboratory techniques and safety that our wet lab team carries out every year. To streamline the training process, the human practices and wet lab team collaborated to create a video series (1-5 minutes per video) on basic techniques, such that members can familiarize themselves with asynchronously and look back to for review. ## <a id="3fdd65dd82be807b971ee54571a28c9f" />Youreka Vancouver Symposium

<a id="3fdd65dd82be804c9933fecfa6766d21" />Youreka is a program that pairs high school students with undergraduate mentors across universities in Canada to create research projects and present them at regional symposiums. The human practices team gave a keynote speech at the Youreka Vancouver Symposium to introduce the high school participants to the UBC iGEM team, garner interest in synthetic biology, and connect our project to their research topic on drugs.

## <a id="3fdd65dd82be80bd8c4ec01fca4d5b72" />UBC Science 101

<a id="3fdd65dd82be8019a426d01d8f4ee7ba" />Expanding our audience to the adult population, Science 101 is a free course offered by UBC’s Faculty of Science to residents of Vancouver’s Downtown Eastside and surrounding communities who are interested in learning about science. We collaborated with the teaching team to deliver a hands-on tutorial to synthetic biology and BioBrick design. The workshop, “Building BioBricks”, introduces students to basic concepts of genetic engineering, plasmid design, and the iGEM BioBrick, building on their previous lecture topics on DNA. Students then grouped into teams, where they used lego bricks to model parts (promoter, ribosome binding site, gene of interest, and terminator) and build their own microplastics biosensor

<a id="3fdd65dd82be804ea2a8e1eb75b567c1" />Our collaboration introduced synthetic biology-related techniques and concepts into one of their tutorial sessions, encouraging specific discussion and introduction to plasmid design and genetically modified organisms.
