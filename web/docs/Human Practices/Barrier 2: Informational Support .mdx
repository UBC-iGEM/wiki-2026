## <a id="3d7d65dd82be80dc8904ebf731ae9864" />Pamphlets

<a id="3d8d65dd82be80768eeced3e200a9f96" />During our literature review, a problem identified was that patients had difficulty finding accessible, reliable information, and patients reported not understanding AMR or their diagnoses, with poor communication from clinicians identified as a contributing factor. This left patients uncertain about their treatments and futures, contributing to psychological strain. Our next step was to see if this problem was consistent with what real patients and healthcare professionals in BC experience through our iHP. Through our stakeholder interviews, recurring themes were brought up that supported this issue:

#### <a id="3ddd65dd82be80438befe30e18573bef" />**Theme A - Patients often don’t understand AMR**

<a id="3ddd65dd82be80c68970e8750a5eacd7" />Infectious disease physician Dr. Richard Lester said that misconceptions were commonly encountered in his practice among patients, with many not understanding what AMR means, thinking that the individual themself is “immune” to antibiotics. In his own practice, Dr. Lester found that explaining the mechanism of illness and treatment directly to patients improved their engagement. This was corroborated by infection control bedside nurse, Lesley Kooner.

#### <a id="3ddd65dd82be80ad9fd9fe8e3f40551b" />**Theme B - Understanding empowers patients**

<a id="3ddd65dd82be8008986dfb7bb7f564c5" />Dr. Lester also brought up the importance of patient self-advocacy in managing their treatment and how taking the time as a clinician to communicate their knowledge can be greatly appreciated and valued by patients. Fellow infectious disease physician Dr. Chan highlighted the importance of patients needing accessible information that they can find outside of their consultations, helping them come prepared with a baseline understanding, allowing them to participate more actively in their healthcare journey.

#### <a id="3f7d65dd82be8036a798ef5b9ef43141" />Implementation:

<a id="3ddd65dd82be808f8495fa622d623a57" />Beyond this insight into healthcare providers’ perspective in BC, the stakeholders gave specific feedback that a powerful way to frame our educational initiatives would be through pamphlets, specifically from Ms. Kooner and Dr. Chan, as they can be easily distributed, visually engaging, and easy to understand. Their specific feedback went into informing the design and information included in our pamphlets. Dr. Chan gave specific examples of common resistant infections commonly spread in BC, like MRSA and VRE, that should be included in the pamphlet, as well as how these infections are spread, environmental cleansing, precautions, and preventative measures patients should take. After creating our first draft, we began a series of DBTL, asking for advice from iHP, such as Lesley Kooner, Norma Washburn, an AMR patient, and Dr. Larry Mroz, who focuses on Patient and Community Engagement in research…(give examples of concrete changes that were made during DBTL)
