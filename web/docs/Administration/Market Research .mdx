<a id="3fcd65dd82be80d0a9b8f378b71bca59" />Now that [we have identified a need, potential customers and determined a minimum prototype](/ubc-vancouver/administration/needs-finding) we shift our focus to the market size and conditions that will govern our product. In order to build a plausible business around *FAST*, we need to answer four main questions. How large is the current market? Which customers should we serve first? Who are our competitors? What rules govern a product like our own? This page outlines our process for answering these critical questions, and sets out where *FAST* fits in the current market, what it must do better, and what it will take to get *FAST* to patients.

# <a id="3ecd65dd82be808da549ecffdb7b1ac3" />Market Sizing

<a id="3fcd65dd82be802a81b9fd74659c4773" />Market sizing helps us judge whether *FAST* can support a sustainable business, and helps us set realistic targets for early growth. We use a common market sizing framework (TAM-PAM-SAM-SOM) to help us determine this.

# <a id="3ecd65dd82be80599055f642055fdd16" />Beachhead Market

<a id="3fcd65dd82be80ce8b21dfafbeae573e" />Now that we have ascertained the potential market out there, we need a place to start. A beachhead market is a narrow, well-defined first group of customers that we can serve well before expanding. Starting with a small group of customers allows us to validate *FAST* in real settings while earning early revenue, credibility, and building client connections.

<a id="3fcd65dd82be8061ba09d1747a152257" />For *FAST*, we propose an initial beachhead market of community primary care clinics in British Columbia (family physician clinics, urgent care clinics, and community pharmacies) for the detection of resistant UTI infections. We chose this group for three main reasons:

- <a id="3fcd65dd82be8000a078c2451bc02def" />**A high volume problem with little testing:** As mentioned in our Problem Statement, UTIs are among the most common bacterial infections, and because of the length of conventional susceptibility testing, most patients are started on antibiotics before results are available.
- <a id="3fcd65dd82be800bbbb4d34c61b60c4c" />**An underserved prescriber:** Since June 2023, pharmacists in BC have been able to prescribe for uncomplicated UTIs, but have been met by physician pushback due to the lack of lab testing available to pharmacists <Cite refs={[{"n":1,"key":"healthPharmacistsCanPrescribe2023"},{"n":2,"key":"emailPharmacistsNowPrescribing2023"}]} />. *FAST* would help to alleviate these concerns and increase prescriber confidence in their chosen course of action.
- <a id="3fcd65dd82be80a58de5cb4daa98b15b" />**Lower costs:** Analyzer-based tests can achieve the rapidity needed for fast decisions (see our Competitor Analysis below), but require large upfront capital investments which only dedicated clinics and hospitals can justify. Pharmacies and small clinics stand to benefit from a low-cost testing method.

<a id="3ecd65dd82be80fca878dbc30c276157" />Success in this beachhead market would provide us with early revenue and the clinical validation needed to pursue expansion.

<a id="3fcd65dd82be8014832ae845373969c4" />Across the competitive landscape, existing solutions fall into three groups: conventional lab testing is cheap but slow, automated lab systems are faster but still require a microbiology lab, and rapid point-of-care tests are fast but rely on expensive dedicated instruments. *FAST* aims to fill the gap between them: susceptibility information within the patient’s first treatment window, at a cost cheaper than conventional testing, without needing a dedicated lab or an expensive proprietary analyzer.

# <a id="3ecd65dd82be802cad4ad58f5437dc1d" />USP Determination

<a id="3fcd65dd82be8005bfdcf34b6ea3f027" />Based on our competitor analysis, we believe *FAST’s* unique selling point to be the ability to offer antibiotic susceptibility testing in the point-of-care setting, without requiring a dedicated microbiology lab or expensive equipment. *FAST* is not the fastest test on the market: analyzer-based systems such as the PA-100 and QolorPhAST return results in under an hour, while *FAST’s* current design requires an incubation step and returns a result within 24 hours. Instead, *FAST* competes on cost and access. Its low-cost reader and single-use chips put susceptibility information within reach of the pharmacies and family clinics that cannot justify the capital cost of a dedicated analyzer, and its result arrives within the patient’s first treatment window, well ahead of the 48 to 72 hours required for conventional culture. Because the biosensor core is shared across antibiotic classes, the same reader can also support new test panels as they are developed. We also recognize the limits of this approach. *FAST* detects antibiotic inactivation, so it does not identify resistance caused by target mutation or efflux, and it is best positioned as a first-line check alongside conventional culture rather than a replacement for it.

# <a id="3ecd65dd82be8048b376d1ec6dee8ed0" />Regulatory Environment

<a id="3fcd65dd82be80c3a692dd1246671838" />As we plan to release our product on the market, we have developed FAST with both domestic and international regulatory considerations to allow us to sell our product in multiple jurisdictions around the world. As we are using a **biosensor** in our test chip, we need to ensure we follow biosafety guidelines, especially regulations around genetically modified organisms. As a medical device, FAST needs to follow the guidelines outlined by Health Canada for approved use in diagnostic settings. As we are using the product with potential AMR candidates, we also considered the Canadian Antimicrobial Resistance Surveillance System and the Human Pathogens and Toxins Act to control the spread of pathogens that are resistant to antibiotics. Since the chip is also handled in a laboratory setting, FAST was designed with the Canadian Biosafety Standard in mind to ensure safe handling and disposal of patient samples and potential AMR candidates.

<a id="3fcd65dd82be80709f06dd34f3cd65c8" />As we strive for international release of our product, FAST was designed with the core values of the World Health Organization’s (WHO) Antimicrobial Resistance Action Plan. The Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety and the International Organization for Standardization’s guidance on medical devices were also used to help guide our product across different regulatory frameworks across the world to ensure global product compliance.

## <a id="3ecd65dd82be807fa4a9f0dde76a6d8d" />International Regulations

### <a id="3ecd65dd82be8090a96cf9f7714fb8cf" />Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety

<a id="3ecd65dd82be80a8bf74d74d39eed701" />The Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety is an international agreement that governs the safe handling, transport, and use of living modified organisms (LMOs). Because FAST utilizes genetically engineered bacterial biosensors, containment measures must be implemented to prevent accidental environmental release. Although FAST is intended for use in controlled healthcare settings, the protocol highlights the importance of biosafety practices when working with engineered microorganisms. It is important to note that this protocol is added specifically for releasing FAST internationally, as we plan to sell FAST across multiple markets across the world, and this protocol will generally help us abide by most biosafety protocols in every market.

### <a id="3ecd65dd82be805ebb3be9796552c00c" />World Health Organization (WHO) Antimicrobial Resistance Action Plan

<a id="3ecd65dd82be808a87e0d9ca60c16981" />The WHO identifies antimicrobial resistance (AMR) as one of the most significant global public health threats. Through the Global Action Plan on Antimicrobial Resistance, member states are encouraged to improve antimicrobial stewardship, strengthen surveillance systems, and support the development of rapid diagnostic technologies that reduce inappropriate antibiotic prescribing.

<a id="3ecd65dd82be8055b074e3a6d053f991" />As a rapid antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST) platform, FAST aligns with international efforts to improve antibiotic stewardship by enabling healthcare providers to select effective treatments earlier and reduce unnecessary antibiotic use.

### <a id="3ecd65dd82be8078a1f2c6798198ebd0" />International Organization for Standardization (ISO)

<a id="3ecd65dd82be809ca694f4e4e676434e" />Medical diagnostic devices are commonly developed under internationally recognized standards. ISO 13485 sets out the requirements for a medical device quality management system, and ISO 14971 defines the risk management process that our own risk assessment is modelled on. For a susceptibility test specifically, ISO 20776-2 describes how the performance of an AST device is evaluated against reference broth microdilution, which is the benchmark *FAST’s* clinical performance study would need to meet. ISO 23640 additionally governs how the stability of in vitro diagnostic reagents is evaluated, which is particularly relevant given that *FAST* relies on living biosensors. Compliance with these standards supports regulatory approval and market adoption across multiple jurisdictions.

## <a id="3ecd65dd82be80aaa2a7d94ea5558f48" />Canadian Regulations

### <a id="3ecd65dd82be80faab3bdcb67f227714" />Food and Drugs Act and Medical Device Regulations

<a id="3ecd65dd82be80219fe1f8e6531eb754" />FAST would likely be regulated by Health Canada as an **In Vitro Diagnostic Medical Device (IVD)** because it analyzes patient samples and provides information used to guide clinical treatment decisions. Because these results directly influence antibiotic prescribing, the device would likely fall within a moderate-risk classification (Class II or III, depending on its final intended use). Before commercialization, FAST would need to be classified by Health Canada and supported by a quality management system, evidence of safety and effectiveness, and a clinical performance study showing agreement with reference susceptibility testing on patient specimens. Together, these form the basis of the Medical Device Licence application required before FAST can be sold in Canada.

### <a id="3ecd65dd82be80929898e4090164a9d7" />Human Pathogens and Toxins Act (HPTA)

<a id="3ecd65dd82be8071af54d54a48a8bcdd" />Because FAST handles clinical bacterial samples that may contain antibiotic-resistant pathogens, development and testing activities must comply with the Human Pathogens and Toxins Act. This legislation regulates the possession, handling, storage, transport, and disposal of human pathogens, as well as the containment requirements and biosafety practices expected of the laboratories that work with them.

### <a id="3ecd65dd82be80179e80d44c18a99547" />Canadian Antimicrobial Resistance Surveillance System (CARSS)

<a id="3ecd65dd82be805eb6d2f3332e73b308" />The Government of Canada actively supports initiatives that improve antimicrobial stewardship through programs such as the Canadian Antimicrobial Resistance Surveillance System (CARSS). Rapid AST technologies align with these national AMR strategies by reducing inappropriate antibiotic prescriptions, supporting evidence-based treatment decisions, and improving surveillance of resistant pathogens.

## <a id="3ecd65dd82be8027bacfe18872196fbd" />Biosafety Regulations

### <a id="3ecd65dd82be8010a449f3cfd94cecc5" />Canadian Biosafety Standard (CBS)

<a id="3ecd65dd82be80308849caf48eb92f39" />The Canadian Biosafety Standard establishes containment requirements for laboratories handling human pathogens.

<a id="3ecd65dd82be800c9041d605268d502b" />Development and validation of FAST would likely involve Risk Group 2 organisms, specifically the bacteria that commonly cause urinary tract infections, such as *Escherichia coli*, *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, and *Enterococcus faecalis*. Laboratories working with these organisms require appropriate containment facilities, personnel biosafety training, decontamination procedures, and biohazardous waste management, as set out in the [Canadian Biosafety Standard, Third Edition](https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/canadian-biosafety-standards-guidelines/third-edition.html), which regulates Risk Group 2, 3, and 4 human pathogens and toxins. Because patient samples may contain antibiotic-resistant bacteria, these practices are especially important for FAST.

### <a id="3ecd65dd82be80df8679f82ac4944c59" />Canadian Environmental Protection Act (CEPA)

<a id="3ecd65dd82be80e4b26cc8d0458ca165" />Because FAST’s biosensors are genetically engineered microorganisms, a commercial product containing them would also fall under the New Substances Notification Regulations (Organisms) of the Canadian Environmental Protection Act. These regulations require companies to submit safety data and undergo a pre-market notification and risk assessment for new microbial products.

<a id="3ecd65dd82be807ea59ff7f8f7089d4c" />Because viable bacteria are contained within the microfluidic chip during testing, safe handling and disposal protocols would be critical for clinical implementation.
