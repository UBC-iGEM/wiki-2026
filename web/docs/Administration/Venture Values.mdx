# Problem Statement

By 2050, a projected 39 million deaths will be due to antimicrobial resistance. This is one of the greatest modern healthcare concerns of our time. With increasing resistance within populations, new and deadly mutagenic strains of pathogens are being spread. Common antibiotics are becoming less effective when treating patients, leading to an increased risks of infection and mortality. While antibiotic susceptibility testing is available in clinics, current methods are slow and only used for high-risk diseases.
\*FAST \*aims to decrease the risk of prescribing a patient with a drug that is no longer able to treat the ailment. Utilising a microfluidic chip model, \*FAST \*is able to identify the resistance of individual pathogens to current antibiotics. Through this, healthcare providers will be able to conduct antibiotic suscetibility testing faster than ever, saving countless of lives.
[https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(24)01867-1/fulltext](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736\(24\)01867-1/fulltext)

# Product Definition

\*FAST  \*is a microfluidic chip with a fluorescent biosensor that is able to detect when a pathogen is resistant to antibiotics. By leveraging antibiotic-related stress and rapid fluorescent detection, we are able to rapidly decide a suitable treatment for patients with urinary tract infections (UTIs). This allows healthcare providers to prescribe medications with confidence and a decreased risk of complications associated with infection. By creating an afforable and accesible way to test antibiotic sensitivity, \*FAST  \*pioneers as a solution to the growing public health concern.

# MVP Determination

## Wet Lab

Our Wet Lab team’s goal is to engineer two biosensory strains **(CpxP-GFP and RecA-GFP reporters)** capable of fluorescing in response to antibiotic-induced stress from **beta-lactams** and **aminoglycosides**. Our team determined two conditions to demonstrate a minimum viable product (MVP).

1. **Biosensor Fluorescence Validation**

This includes introducing a reporter plasmid to a biosensory strain and confirming uptake without impeding cell growth and viability.
This further includes validating that the biosensor produces fluorescence signals when exposed to degraded antibiotics. The signals should be distinguishable from negative controls.

1. **Characterizing the MIC range and maximum threshold of antibiotic exposure**

This is done by titrating the MIC range of the biosensors and WT Epi300 (no reporter plasmid), thus demonstrating the maximum threshold of antibiotic exposure.

## Dry Lab

Our Dry Lab team is focused on engineering functional **bioreactors** to increase E. coli production and create a **microfluidic chip** and **fluorescence detection system** to support the production and structural deployment of our project. Two key MVP conditions guide our efforts:

1. **Bioreactor Functionality**
   - Culturing E. coli at a rate comparable to or exceeding that of traditional lab flask methods under controlled conditions. This E. coli is then used for the biosensory developmet in antibiotic susceptibility testing.
   - Improving the current features of the bioreactor
2. **Microfluidic Chip Production**
   - Utilizing PDMS molds to create a microfluidic chip used for precise fluid control.
   - The microfluidic chip is designed to process a patient's sample and evaluate bacterial antibiotic susceptibility.
