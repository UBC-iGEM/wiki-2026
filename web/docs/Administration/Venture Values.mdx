# Problem Statement

By 2050, a projected 39 million deaths will be due to antimicrobial resistance. This is one of the greatest modern healthcare concerns of our time. With increasing resistance within populations, new and deadly mutagenic strains of pathogens are being spread. Common antibiotics are becoming less effective when treating patients, leading to an increased risks of infection and mortality. While antibiotic susceptibility testing is available in clinics, current methods are slow and only used for high-risk diseases.
\*FAST \*aims to decrease the risk of prescribing a patient with a drug that is no longer able to treat the ailment. Utilising a microfluidic chip model, \*FAST \*is able to identify the resistance of individual pathogens to current antibiotics. Through this, healthcare providers will be able to conduct antibiotic suscetibility testing faster than ever, saving countless of lives.

# Product Definition

\*FAST  \*is a microfluidic chip with a fluorescent biosensor that is able to detect when a pathogen is resistant to antibiotics. By leveraging antibiotic-related stress and rapid fluorescent detection, we are able to rapidly decide a suitable treatment for patients with urinary tract infections (UTIs). This allows healthcare providers to prescribe medications with confidence and a decreased risk of complications associated with infection. By creating an afforable and accesible way to test antibiotic sensitivity, \*FAST  \*pioneers as a solution to the growing public health concern.

# Problem Statement

Our planet’s construction industry is unsustainable, with concrete production emitting 8% of global CO2 while mining waste contaminates ecosystems with heavy metals. At the same time, the rapidly expanding space economy faces a critical barrier: the lack of scalable, self-sustaining infrastructure for long-term extraterrestrial habitation. Current space construction relies on costly, Earth-dependent supply chains, which are impractical for large-scale interplanetary bases, orbital stations, or deep-space missions.

# Product Definition

\*CyanoBricks \*addresses both challenges with a single biotechnological breakthrough in engineering microorganisms. On Earth, our cyanobacteria-based biomaterial captures $CO_2$ to produce carbon-negative bio-cement, stabilizing hazardous mine waste and reducing reliance on virgin resources. In space, the same technology transforms local regolith and atmospheric CO2 into self-repairing, radiation-resistant structures enabling *in-situ* resource utilization (ISRU) for sustainable off-world construction. By turning two pressing resource crises into circular solutions, \*CyanoBricks \*pioneers a future where sustainable building on Earth and in space are one and the same.
*CyanoBricks* is a biotechnology platform that transforms mining waste into sustainable, carbon-negative biomaterial concrete. By leveraging engineering Cyanobacteria, we develop bio-based construction materials that replace traditional cement, reducing carbon dioxide emissions, detoxifying hazardous mine tailings, and enabling circular resource use.

Stakeholders on Earth—such as mining companies, environmental organizations, and construction businesses—are looking for cost-efficient and sustainable approaches that enhance environmental results and ensure adherence to regulations. They gain advantages through lower remediation expenses, better public and investor perception, and the development of new revenue opportunities by utilizing locally sourced, eco-friendly construction materials. Implementing sustainable practices also aids in achieving certification objectives (like LEED or BREEAM), decreases emissions, and fosters long-term ecosystem health and community trust.

Traditional mining and construction encounter significant costs for remediation and materials, as well as environmental liabilities, and they often lack effective solutions. The enforcement of regulations is challenging and inconsistent, while sustainable materials frequently suffer from issues related to affordability, availability, and consistent quality. Additionally, public pressure, legal uncertainties, and the complexities of adopting new green technologies further obstruct progress. The Earth's environment is adversely affected by toxic waste, soil and water pollution, and enduring damage resulting from outdated industrial methods.

Environmental, industrial and governmental stakeholders must co-manage tailings, reduce environmental harm, and meet evolving regulations while improving efficiency and reputation. They are tasked with sourcing sustainable materials, protecting public health, enhancing long-term performance, and collaborating across sectors to implement innovative, responsible solutions. Agencies must ensure transparency, incentivize eco-practices, and align industrial operations with Earth’s ecological boundaries and sustainability goals.
