<a id="3fdd65dd82be80b8b341c44efeb074d1" />While [Strategic Planning](/ubc-vancouver/administration/strategic-planning) and [Business Development ](/ubc-vancouver/administration/business-development-) set our how *FAST* would reach its first customers in BC, this page takes a longer view. Here, we cover how *FAST* would be funded as it grows, how production and distribution would be scaled, and what lasting societal impacts *FAST* could have.

# <a id="359d65dd82be817abeefeac74ee60a7d" />Growth Strategy

<a id="359d65dd82be81709a85c2daf4267006" />FAST’s growth pathway can be understood as a sequence of funding stages, each designed to retire a specific type of risk. Early capital is used to prove that the science works. Later capital is used to prove that the product can be manufactured, approved, paid for, and adopted. Because investors take on less risk at each stage, each successive round can be larger and less dilutive than the last. This makes the order in which FAST answers its key questions as important as the answers themselves.

<br id="3ead65dd82be805d9dfbce3152d4b95f" />

<details>
  <summary><a id="3fdd65dd82be80f49a4bcd8c906fc443" />**1. Bootstrapping (2026):** Institutional Sponsors</summary>

  <a id="3fdd65dd82be802abda5f0f1027d1123" />To date, FAST has been bootstrapped: developed using the resources already available to the team rather than outside investment. Funding has come entirely from institutional sponsors, alongside university lab facilities and the time and expertise of team members. This has allowed the team to explore the idea and generate early results without giving up ownership. At this stage, the central question is whether biosensor bacteria can reliably report antibiotic inactivation within a microfluidic chip. The team defines the problem with clinicians and stakeholders, builds and characterizes the first biosensors and chip, and documents designs and experimental data. Critically, the team also files its first patent application at this stage, before further public disclosure. Securing intellectual property first ensures that every later stage builds on protected ground, rather than on an idea that others could freely claim.
</details>

<details>
  <summary><a id="3fdd65dd82be801dbaa3c51371e15964" />**2. Non‑Dilutive Funding (2026–2028):** Grants and Programs</summary>

  <a id="3fdd65dd82be80ee9085e11322cebaa6" />The next stage relies on non‑dilutive funding: money that does not require giving up ownership. Programs such as [Mitacs](https://www.mitacs.ca/), [NRC IRAP](https://nrc.canada.ca/en/support-technology-innovation/financial-support-technology-innovation), [New Ventures BC](https://www.newventuresbc.com/competition/), and CIHR research grants, along with [CARB‑X](https://carb-x.org/), which funds products against drug‑resistant bacteria including diagnostics, could carry FAST from proof of concept to an integrated chip‑and‑reader prototype. The question shifts from whether the science works to whether it can work outside the lab, in a device a clinician could use. Because this capital comes without equity, the team keeps full ownership through the period when FAST is at its riskiest and least valuable. That ownership is what gives the team bargaining power in later negotiations with investors.
</details>

<details>
  <summary><a id="3fdd65dd82be803b9267f0f9e1491698" />**3. Seed Stage (2027–2028):** Angel Investors</summary>

  <a id="3fdd65dd82be805cb2c2d235615f645b" />With intellectual property filed and a working prototype in hand, FAST becomes attractive to angel investors and early‑stage funds, who invest their own capital in exchange for equity or convertible notes. Seed funding addresses the practical challenges that separate a prototype from a product: stabilizing living biosensors so they survive storage without refrigeration, building biocontainment into the cartridge, and establishing an ISO 13485 quality management system. Beyond capital, angel investors often bring mentorship and networks that help a scientific team learn to operate as a company. For a team with a research background, this is often as valuable as the funding itself.
</details>

<details>
  <summary><a id="3fdd65dd82be80a1aad9fbede40e38ac" />**4. Series A (2028–2030):** Venture Capital</summary>

  <a id="3fdd65dd82be80d48718d932277eb2a5" />With a validated prototype and patent protection in place, FAST can seek Series A funding from venture capital firms specializing in diagnostics or health technology. This is the most demanding stage of FAST’s growth, because it must answer two questions at once. Is FAST safe and effective enough for Health Canada to license it? And is it valuable enough for a provincial health plan to pay for it? Series A therefore funds the clinical performance study, the medical device licence application, paid pilots in BC clinics and pharmacies, and the health‑economic evidence needed for provincial coverage. Investors at this stage are betting not only on the technology, but on FAST’s ability to navigate a healthcare system where the user and the payer are not the same.
</details>

<details>
  <summary><a id="3fdd65dd82be808bb649c76319543764" />**5. Series B (2030–2032):** Commercial Launch and Expansion</summary>

  <a id="3fdd65dd82be80d7bb19f9f594ca1c02" />Once FAST is licensed and launched in British Columbia, Series B funding supports the scale‑up of cartridge manufacturing, the building of a commercial team, and expansion into other provinces as coverage decisions follow. Here the question becomes whether FAST can be adopted at scale and sustain itself financially. Early commercial years are expected to operate at a loss as the installed base of readers grows. This round carries FAST through that period until recurring cartridge revenue is sufficient to reach profitability.
</details>

<details>
  <summary><a id="3fdd65dd82be801ba8a3e7d576a1fd5b" />**6. Growth Stage (2033 onward):** Platform and International Expansion</summary>

  <a id="3fdd65dd82be80ec9bc2fb552d1ea535" />With established revenue, FAST can fund further growth through later investment rounds, strategic partnerships, or reinvested profit. At this stage, FAST’s platform design becomes its greatest asset. New cartridge panels for other resistance mechanisms and infections can be offered on readers already in clinics, and international single‑payer markets can be approached with an adapted version of the evidence package built for Canada. The platform can also be licensed for veterinary and agricultural use, extending its reach into markets FAST would not enter directly. It is also at this stage that the question of who should carry FAST forward, discussed under Exit Strategies, becomes relevant.
</details>

<a id="3fdd65dd82be80029ea0c201c0d85d03" />Progressing through these stages shows how FAST could grow from early validation into broad clinical adoption. Each stage builds credibility, partnerships, and capital for the next, and no stage is attempted before the one beneath it is secure. By pairing scientific milestones with a clear funding roadmap, FAST positions itself as a venture capable of improving how antibiotics are prescribed in clinical practice.

# <a id="3fdd65dd82be80f1a1a6cb471329e0a4" />Scalability Assessment

<a id="3fdd65dd82be80a08d26c43a8bc84c02" />Scaling FAST presents an unusual challenge. It means scaling two very different things at once: a living biological product and a regulated medical device. Each has its own constraints, and FAST’s long‑term success depends on managing both together.

## <a id="3fdd65dd82be808f9c11ca90eb7163f5" />1. Manufacturing

<a id="3fdd65dd82be80e0a469da16ac44291a" />The three components of FAST scale in different ways:

<a id="3fdd65dd82be803ea02fc87f27263861" />

| <a id="3fdd65dd82be8040b6a4f49a4b7c50ef" />Component                   | Approach to scale                                                                                                                                       | Key considerations                                                                                             |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| <a id="3fdd65dd82be80dd9f84db3560fa606c" />**Biosensor strains**       | Produced in‑house by standard *E. coli* fermentation, a mature and inexpensive process, then stabilized (e.g., freeze‑dried) and loaded into cartridges | Batch‑to‑batch consistency, shelf life without cold storage, and quality control release testing for every lot |
| <a id="3fdd65dd82be8062a0fadc45ee46c742" />**Microfluidic cartridges** | Transitioned from lab fabrication to injection moulding through a contract manufacturer, reducing cost per unit as volume increases                     | Design for manufacturability, and sealing cartridges so engineered bacteria remain contained throughout use    |
| <a id="3fdd65dd82be8054a025fc9ac9586e28" />**Fluorescence reader**     | Produced by an electronics contract manufacturer once the design is finalized                                                                           | Component supply chains and electrical safety certification                                                    |

<a id="3fdd65dd82be80cf8c18c797feb727f8" />Keeping biosensor production in‑house protects both quality and trade secrets in the part of FAST that is hardest to replicate. Contracting out cartridge and reader manufacturing allows FAST to scale without the cost of building its own facilities. All manufacturing would operate under an ISO 13485 quality management system.

## <a id="3fdd65dd82be8010a8d4fddf0e2619ab" />2. Distribution

<a id="3fdd65dd82be80f1bf06d164c6775157" />In British Columbia, FAST would be sold directly to family physician clinics and community pharmacies. This allows the team to work closely with early adopters, learn from real‑world use, and refine the product before wider release. As provincial coverage is secured, distribution would shift toward health authority procurement.

<a id="3fdd65dd82be80a4b5c2eab0b52e96cf" />Beyond BC, FAST would partner with established medical device distributors whose regulatory expertise, logistics networks, and customer relationships would be costly to replicate. Distribution must also include the end of each cartridge’s life. Used cartridges would be returned or disposed of under a defined biohazard protocol, so that containment of engineered bacteria extends beyond the clinic.

## <a id="3fdd65dd82be80c7ad7ec12ec67e4730" />3. Expansion to Other Markets

<a id="3fdd65dd82be80a298a9fc3919af5bc4" />Because FAST is a platform rather than a single test, expansion does not require a new device. New biosensors for other resistance enzymes, antibiotic classes, and bacterial infections can be offered as new cartridge types on readers already in use, which increases the value of every reader placed. However, each new panel must be independently validated and approved, so expansion is gradual rather than immediate. Beyond human healthcare, the platform could be licensed for veterinary and agricultural antibiotic testing, where antimicrobial resistance presents the same challenge under a One Health approach.

# <a id="359d65dd82be818c91b1ce37a3492f7a" />Sustainability Considerations

<a id="3fdd65dd82be80d1acc1cbf8d8f06e6f" />

<Img url="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6279/wiki/assets/279e22669c3f57e980f724622eff75a4.svg" alt="" scale={0} align="center" />

## <a id="3ead65dd82be80ba9a59e83357081006" />Goal 3: Good Health and Well-being

<a id="3ead65dd82be8017bc34cf0710edfc19" />**Ensure healthy lives and promote well-being for all at all ages.**

<a id="3ead65dd82be8011b133e9ddd491931a" />FAST supports point‑of‑care antibiotic susceptibility testing (AST), helping healthcare providers determine whether a chosen antibiotic remains effective against a patient’s bacterial infection. By providing actionable results at the point of care rather than days later from a centralized laboratory, FAST could reduce ineffective antibiotic prescribing, support timely and targeted treatment, and improve patient outcomes. However, because FAST detects antibiotic inactivation rather than resistance caused by target mutation or efflux, over‑reliance on a susceptible result could still lead to treatment failure in some patients. Clear communication of the test’s scope, along with its use as a first‑line check alongside conventional culture, will be essential to ensuring that more informed antibiotic use supports antimicrobial stewardship and efforts to address antimicrobial resistance (AMR) in the long term.

## <a id="3ead65dd82be80e0ba5fdefc96933c55" />Goal 8: Decent Work and Economic Growth

<a id="3ead65dd82be8006a274cb90910e330b" />**Promote sustained, inclusive, and sustainable economic growth, full and productive employment, and decent work for all.**

<a id="3ead65dd82be8074bc8ccd72e1cb16fe" />Effective diagnostics have the potential to reduce the economic burden associated with prolonged infections, repeat visits, and ineffective treatments. As FAST progresses toward commercialization and larger‑scale production, it could also contribute to innovation‑driven economic activity and create skilled employment opportunities in areas such as biotechnology research and development, manufacturing, quality assurance, and regulatory affairs. At the same time, point‑of‑care testing shifts work from centralized laboratories to family physicians and pharmacists, who already face significant workloads. Designing FAST to fit within a standard appointment, and ensuring that the time required for testing is recognized in how it is funded, will be important to ensuring these economic benefits do not come at the expense of frontline healthcare workers.

## <a id="3ead65dd82be8045bb16f4a628e81c43" />Goal 9: Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure

<a id="3ead65dd82be80d18fc8d7ed847afe66" />**Build resilient infrastructure, promote inclusive and sustainable industrialization, and foster innovation.**

<a id="3ead65dd82be806ea19fc041ab9b93b2" />FAST contributes to healthcare innovation through the development of a point‑of‑care diagnostic platform that combines microfluidics with synthetic biology biosensors, and its open BioBrick parts allow others to build on this work. The novelty of the platform also presents challenges, as a clinical device that relies on live engineered bacteria may face a longer regulatory pathway and greater public scepticism than conventional diagnostics. Early engagement with Health Canada, built‑in biocontainment, and transparent communication about how engineered organisms are contained will therefore be as important to FAST’s contribution as the technology itself. Continued research, validation, and commercialization of the platform could further contribute to innovation within the biotechnology and diagnostics industries.

## <a id="3ead65dd82be80e0b82cdd5795d68834" />Goal 10: Reduced Inequalities

<a id="3ead65dd82be809384ead63214a61008" />**Reduce inequality within and among countries.**

<a id="3ead65dd82be80a79042effef40e4ebf" />FAST has the potential to improve access to timely antibiotic susceptibility testing by developing a low‑cost and portable diagnostic platform. Conventional AST can require specialized laboratory infrastructure and extended turnaround times, which may limit timely access to susceptibility information in rural, remote, and resource‑limited settings. However, new health technologies are often adopted first by well‑resourced urban facilities, and without deliberate planning, FAST could widen existing gaps rather than close them. In lower‑income countries, the need for a reader, reliable power, and a consistent supply of cartridges could also limit access despite a low cost per test. Prioritizing underserved sites during expansion, pursuing public coverage, and partnering with global health organizations could help ensure FAST expands access where it is needed most.

## <a id="3ead65dd82be8005bad8dcd837156acd" />Goal 12: Responsible Consumption and Production

<a id="3ead65dd82be8073aec2fd978c13c4fc" />**Ensure sustainable consumption and production patterns.**

<a id="3ead65dd82be806698eee0cc6ddffb96" />FAST supports more responsible antibiotic use by providing healthcare providers with timely information about whether a chosen antibiotic will be effective against a patient’s infection. This can help reduce unnecessary or ineffective antibiotic use, promote more targeted prescribing, and preserve the effectiveness of antibiotics for future patients. However, each test relies on a single‑use plastic cartridge that becomes biohazard waste, and at scale this could contribute significantly to medical waste. Minimizing cartridge materials, exploring recyclable or lower‑impact alternatives, and establishing defined disposal programs will be necessary to ensure that FAST’s contribution to responsible antibiotic use is not offset by its own environmental footprint.

## <a id="3ead65dd82be806a8485cdf8f4117e15" />Goal 17: Partnerships for the Goals

<a id="3ead65dd82be8050a43ad03fa9d84c05" />**Strengthen the means of implementation and revitalize the Global Partnership for Sustainable Development.**

<a id="3ead65dd82be8006a27ffe25267a6912" />The development and implementation of FAST relies on collaboration across multiple disciplines and sectors. By engaging with healthcare providers, researchers, industry experts, regulatory stakeholders, and potential commercialization partners, the team can incorporate diverse expertise into the development of the technology and help ensure that it reflects real‑world healthcare needs. As FAST grows, however, commercial partners and investors may place greater emphasis on the most profitable markets rather than those with the greatest need. Building access commitments into licensing and partnership agreements from the outset could help ensure that FAST’s mission is maintained as it transitions from a research project into a clinically relevant diagnostic tool.

# <a id="3fdd65dd82be80b9a7a7d8b1ffdb685e" />Exit Strategies

<a id="3fdd65dd82be80a1aa78c93e6a7a0c0c" />The final question in FAST’s growth is who should carry it forward. An exit allows founders and early investors to realize the value they helped create. For a diagnostic built around antimicrobial stewardship and access, the choice of exit also determines whether that purpose survives the transition. The options below are therefore weighed not only on financial return, but on what each would mean for FAST’s mission.

<a id="3fdd65dd82be80e1b415dda582277638" />

| <a id="3fdd65dd82be804482caebfb6964b2c5" />Exit Strategy                                          | Benefits                                                                                                                                                | Considerations                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| <a id="3fdd65dd82be80b09821f6392151da32" />**Acquisition by a Diagnostics or Healthcare Company** | Existing regulatory, manufacturing, clinical, and distribution infrastructure; fastest route to large‑scale adoption                                    | Requires strong clinical and economic validation; acquisition terms; risk that the acquirer deprioritizes FAST or its access commitments |
| <a id="3fdd65dd82be806c8800c5817ae68ca0" />**Technology Licensing**                               | Recurring royalties while retaining IP ownership; potential for multiple partners across markets and sectors, including veterinary and agricultural use | Requires strong IP protection; licensing and quality‑control agreements; continued technical support for licensees                       |
| <a id="3fdd65dd82be80f69f5ac7326eb4cf5c" />**Independent Growth Backed by Healthcare Investors**  | Retains control over FAST’s direction and mission; builds long‑term value as new panels and markets are added                                           | Longer time to returns for investors; ownership dilution across funding rounds; requires building a full commercial organization         |
| <a id="3fdd65dd82be80ffb566ecc478b7c8bc" />**Initial Public Offering (IPO)**                      | Significant capital for global expansion and research; liquidity for early investors                                                                    | Only realistic at substantial scale; high regulatory and reporting costs; public‑market pressure toward short‑term results               |

<a id="3fdd65dd82be80ba85dcf8188712c41a" />No single exit strategy is ideal at every stage. In the near term, independent growth allows FAST to prove its clinical and economic value on its own terms, while licensing extends its reach into markets the company would not enter directly. Acquisition becomes a stronger option once that value is established, as FAST would then be in a position to negotiate terms that protect its access commitments. Whichever path is chosen, the guiding principle remains the same: FAST should grow in a way that keeps antibiotics working for the patients who need them.
