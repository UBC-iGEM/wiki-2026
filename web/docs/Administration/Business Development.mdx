# Marketing Strategy

Our marketing strategy follows a layered approach to ensure that investor confidence grows in parallel with technological milestones and aligns with early stage funding.
To enter the beachhead market, our company’s first priority is deep market discovery. This includes conducting in depth analyses to identify unmet needs in mining remediation and sustainable construction, while building relationships with early adopters in both industries. At the same time, we pursue early IP protection to secure a first mover advantage, and actively engage with regulatory agencies to establish biosafety, GMO compliance, and ecological release protocols. These foundations are paired with case-study pilots and lab data that validate the real world feasibility of our platform, demonstrating quantifiable cost savings against traditional remediation and construction methods.
The first product we will bring to market in Year 3 is a set of bioink and bioprinter prototypes, validated through laboratory trials with mining waste. These prototypes serve as proof-of-concept demonstrations to attract pilot partners and generate confidence in our technology. Alongside the prototypes, we will also offer pilot project services to test biocementation directly in operational environments.
To achieve this, we require a blend of financial, human, and industrial resources. Seed funding and early venture capital will provide the runway for R\&D and prototype development, complemented by government programs such as Mitacs grants for salaries, equipment, and material support. A dedicated R\&D investment is directed toward refining engineered microbes, bio-ink formulations, and bioprinter platforms, while partnerships with industry are critical to secure waste material access and field testing sites.
To show investors the possible, we emphasize tangible, compelling demonstrations. These include live bioprinting at conferences, visible small scale prototype structures and regolith based demo bricks, and clear communication of the cost savings and environmental benefits compared with traditional remediation and construction approaches.
Our scaling pathway mirrors the commercialization roadmap. In Year 1 to 2, efforts remain focused on in-house research, safety compliance, and prototype design. By Year 3, we deliver prototypes and lab-validated data to partners, followed by pilot scale trials in Year 4 to 5 with mining and construction firms to prove scalability. Year 5 also marks the establishment of a pilot production facility and early distributor and supplier partnerships. From Year 6 onward, the business transitions into commercial sales and licensing, with initial adoption from early construction firms on Earth and eventual expansion into space applications.

# Product Iteration Framework

## Elevator Pitch DBTL

Through the Design-Build-Test-Learn process, our goal was to craft a pitch that not only communicates the urgency of the climate and industrial challenges on Earth but also proves why MeduCA is the most viable solution for extraterrestrial infrastructure.
%% START :: dbtl %%
Design: The Design phase focused on identifying the core context surrounding our initial project problem statements. By relegating the team to focus on both Earthly applications and Space applications, we were able to define key factors like scalability, and relevance in two distinct but interconnected domains.
Build: The Build phase centered on transforming technical aspects into a compelling narrative by first mapping key motivators and pain points across mining, construction, and space sectors for stakeholders. We then, refined core message by creating hypotheses and testing value propositions that ensure clarity across  scientific and non technical audiences. Finally, we performed scrapes on pre-existing business to identify market gaps and competitively position MeduCA.
Test: Through workshops, competitive comparisons, and team reviews, we tested clarity and impact, market relevance, competitive differentiation, and logistical feasibility. We conducted intellectual property research to map the existing competitive landscape in the biocement space. Additionally, we tested our market hypotheses against industry market reports to gather preliminary evidence of market size and identify important stakeholders, including key competitors operating in both terrestrial and space applications.
Learn: We learned that emphasizing the duality of the project and unique market position provides an edge and credibility to our narrative. Our competitive analysis revealed that while several companies operate in the biocement space, none offer custom printable formats: a critical gap in the market. This insight reinforced our strategic direction to position MeduCA not merely as a biocement product, but as an integrated solution offering both bioprinters and bioink. This differentiation became a cornerstone of our value proposition, allowing us to competitively position MeduCA as uniquely capable of delivering customizable, on-demand biomaterial solutions for both Earth-based industries (mining and construction) and space applications where adaptability and precision are paramount.

## Stakeholder Determination DBTL

Understanding who our solution serves and why they care was central to shaping our Stakeholder Determination. Through the Design-Build-Test-Learn process, we systematically identified and validated the key stakeholders across Earth-based industries and space infrastructure development.
%% START :: dbtl %%
During the Design phase, we identified key stakeholder groups by categorizing them by their roles such as Decision Maker, Economic Buyer, Recommender, Influencer, User, and Beneficiary. This structured approach enabled us to determine which groups would be most affected and most influential in the adoption of our technology across both Earth-based and space-based applications.
During the Build phase, we translated stakeholder insights into practical tools and frameworks to act as a guide. We developed customer segmentation tables to define primary markets, mapped tailored value propositions to address specific pains and gains, and created side-by-side matrices stakeholder tables for Earth and space applications.
In the Testing phase, we assessed our stakeholder assumptions through an iterative process, beginning with initial mapping and competitor benchmarking to hypothesize stakeholder motivations and challenges. We reviewed sustainability standards to help tailor our project research, remediation policies, and space regulations. This led to a refined stakeholder framework to focus on potential decision-makers, buyers, and users of MeduCA.
The Testing phase revealed stakeholders value solutions that tackle both environmental and economic challenges. Our dual-purpose approach for Earth and space is unique and addresses unmet needs. In the Learning phase, clear communication of cost savings, risk reduction, and regulatory compliance is critical to gaining support from decision-makers and funders.
