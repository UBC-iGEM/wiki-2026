# Growth Strategy

FAST growth pathway can be understood as a sequence of funding stages that present how the project evolves from a student-led initiative into a biotechnology venture in diagnostic antibiotic susceptibility testing. Each stage introduces new funding sources, milestones, and partnerships that unlock greater opportunities for validation and commercialization.

# 1. Pre-seed Stage: Bootstrapping & 3F

Initially, we plan to fund FAST through personal funding from team members and from friends, family, and early adopters. These early resources allow the team to define project goals, design microbial strains, and carry out preliminary trials on early UTI AST models. Though limited in scale, this stage is essential for building credibility and demonstrating feasibility.

# 2. Seed Stage: Non-Dilutive Funding

The next stage relies on non-dilutive funding, which is money that does not require giving up ownership. Grants from organizations such as Government of Canada, Mitacs, and UBC programs provide the resources to validate AST detection in the lab. Growth here comes from generating technical proof while preserving full ownership of FAST’s innovations.

# 3. Series Seed: Angel Investors

After grant funding, FAST becomes attractive to angel investors. These are individuals who invest their personal money into promising early-stage ventures, often in exchange for equity or convertible debt. Angel funding allows FAST to conduct clinical pilot studies, while also filing for intellectual property (IP) protection on microbial strains and hardware. Growth potential expands at this stage, since outside capital and mentorship help bridge the gap between academic validation and venture capitalism.

# 4. Series A: Venture Capital

With successful pilots and IP in place, FAST can seek Series A funding from venture capital (VC) firms, with professional funds that invest in startups with high growth potential. This capital supports scaling production, pursuing biosafety approvals, and forming early partnerships with biotech and diagnostic companies. The growth potential lies in moving from research and trials to initial commercial contracts.

# 5. Series B: Expansion Funding

Series B provides larger-scale financing to broaden operations, expand into international markets, and hire specialized talent. By this point, FAST AST detection capabilities are validated at a commercial level, and funding is directed toward deploying our product in clinical workflows. Growth accelerates here as FAST proves it can compete with other diagnostic tools.

# 6. Series C: Market Leadership

At the Series C stage, FAST focuses on market leadership. Investors now include large corporate partners. Funding enables licensing agreements, new product lines, and stronger regulatory and intellectual property portfolios. Growth potential comes from securing a leading position across multiple industries and building resilience against competition.

# 7. Series D: Late-stage Growth

Series D represents late-stage financing, where FAST is already established but seeks additional resources for aggressive international expansion, acquisitions, or preparation for public listing. By this stage, FAST has strong revenue streams and broad adoption, maximizing growth potential as it establishes its role as a global leader in diagnostic antibiotic susceptibility testing.

# 8. Initial Public Offering (IPO)

An Initial Public Offering (IPO) occurs when FAST lists shares on a stock exchange and offers them to the public. This step provides significant capital inflow, liquidity for early investors, and worldwide visibility. Growth potential here lies in expanding operations at a global scale, funding long-term research and development, and ensuring FAST’s influence across global clinical workflows.<br/>
Progressing through these funding stages shows how FAST can grow from early validation into large-scale adoption. Each step builds resources, partnerships, and credibility, while opening new technical and financial opportunities. By combining scientific innovation with a clear funding roadmap, FAST positions itself as a sustainable biotechnology venture capable of transforming and redefining patient care and antibiotic susceptibility testing.

# Scalability Assessment

## 1. Centralized manufacturing model

To scale the production of our Caulobacter-based remediation spray, meduCA can adopt a centralized manufacturing model following initial pilot manufacturing facilities. The primary advantage of centralized manufacturing is the ability to achieve significant economies of scale through consolidated production as opposed to decentralized models. meduCA can establish a centralized production facility for the core biological component, the engineered bacteria and bioink. This involves the industrial-scale fermentation of our engineered bacteria (*Caulobacter crescentus* for Earth, *Synechococcus elongatus* for Space) in which key production goals revolves around the optimization of strain, growth conditions, and genetic stability for maximum cell density and enzyme expression at an industrial-scale. Challenges in building a large-scale production include the cost and processing system for necessary specialized equipment, such as bioreactors, bioprinters, sprayers, and deployment kits. For rapid scale, meduCA should partner with a compatible and experienced CMO (Contract Manufacturing Organization) to scale production of hardware and starter kits.

## 2. Commercialization and distribution of product

Following the centralized production of our engineered bacterial and hardware components, distribution is designed for maximum efficiency and market penetration. MeduCA will employ a business-to-business hybrid model, combining direct sales with strategic channel partnerships. A direct sales team would engage with mining firms, green construction companies, sustainable infrastructure firms, national space agencies and research institutions for major high value contracts. Simultaneously, established distributors bring logistics networks, relationships, and experience handling microbial products to reach smaller mining operations and environmental consulting firms.

## 3. Expansion to other markets

Once the core technology and production line is consistent, the platform is adaptable for expansion into other environments or markets. Ocean and aquatic environments serve as an example of an adjacent market following our initial beachhead in biocementation and martian construction. This would involve developing marine-suited formulations of our bacteria to saline environments and shifting the form of application for aquatic environments to a deployable system in water. MeduCA could be designed for applications such as restoring coastal infrastructure and barriers against erosion or constructing artificial reefs, where it serves as a supportive scaffold that mimics complex coral structures. Ultimately, venturing into adjacent markets will require a target market-driven research and development team to reconfigure applications of our microbial platform and delivery mechanism, along with strategic partnerships with target sector organizations.

# Sustainability Considerations

### Goal 3: Good Health and Well-being

**Ensure healthy lives and promote well-being for all at all ages.**
FAST enables rapid antibiotic susceptibility testing (AST), helping healthcare providers identify which antibiotics are effective against a patient's bacterial infection. By providing actionable results more quickly than conventional AST methods, FAST could reduce ineffective antibiotic prescribing, support timely and targeted treatment, and improve patient outcomes. In the long term, more informed antibiotic use can also support antimicrobial stewardship and efforts to address antimicrobial resistance (AMR).

### Goal 8: Decent Work and Economic Growth

**Promote sustained, inclusive, and sustainable economic growth, full and productive employment, and decent work for all.**
Rapid and effective diagnostics have the potential to reduce the economic burden associated with prolonged infections, extended hospital stays, and ineffective treatments. As FAST progresses toward commercialization and larger-scale production, it could also contribute to innovation-driven economic activity and create skilled employment opportunities in areas such as biotechnology research and development, manufacturing, quality assurance, regulatory affairs, and commercialization.

### Goal 9: Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure

**Build resilient infrastructure, promote inclusive and sustainable industrialization, and foster innovation.**
FAST contributes to healthcare innovation through the development of a rapid antibiotic susceptibility testing platform that combines microfluidics with biosensing technology. By working toward a faster and more accessible alternative to conventional AST methods, FAST supports the development and translation of new diagnostic technologies. Continued research, validation, and commercialization of the platform could further contribute to innovation within the biotechnology and diagnostics industries.

### Goal 10: Reduced Inequalities

**Reduce inequality within and among countries.**
FAST has the potential to improve access to timely antibiotic susceptibility testing by developing a rapid and affordable diagnostic platform. Conventional AST can require specialized laboratory infrastructure and extended turnaround times, which may limit timely access to susceptibility information in some healthcare settings. Through future efforts to improve affordability, portability, and accessibility, FAST could help expand access to rapid AST across community hospitals, smaller healthcare facilities, and resource-limited settings.

### Goal 12: Responsible Consumption and Production

**Ensure sustainable consumption and production patterns.**
FAST supports more responsible antibiotic use by providing healthcare providers with timely information about which antibiotics are effective against a patient's infection. This can help reduce unnecessary or ineffective antibiotic use and promote more targeted prescribing. By supporting antimicrobial stewardship, FAST contributes to the responsible use of antibiotics as an important healthcare resource and helps preserve their effectiveness for future patients.

### Goal 17: Partnerships for the Goals

**Strengthen the means of implementation and revitalize the Global Partnership for Sustainable Development.**
The development and implementation of FAST relies on collaboration across multiple disciplines and sectors. By engaging with healthcare providers, researchers, industry experts, regulatory stakeholders, and potential commercialization partners, the team can incorporate diverse expertise into the development of the technology. These partnerships support the translation of FAST from a research project into a clinically relevant diagnostic tool and help ensure that its development reflects real-world healthcare needs.
